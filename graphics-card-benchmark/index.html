// WHAT YOUR SCORE MEANS

Your graphics, in plain English

Integrated / basic

Perfect for everyday work, web, email, video calls and office apps. Modern 3D games and 4K editing will struggle — but that’s rarely what these machines are for.

Entry-level

Comfortable for everyday use, lighter or older games and casual creative work. A dedicated card would lift gaming and creative performance a lot.

Mainstream

A solid all-rounder — 1080p gaming, photo and video editing and most creative apps run well. A great balance of power and value.

High-end

Strong for 1440p gaming, 4K video editing, 3D rendering and demanding creative and engineering work. A genuinely capable machine.

Enthusiast

Top-tier performance for 4K gaming, real-time 3D, video production and local AI workloads. Not much will slow this down.

Score isn’t everything

Drivers, cooling, dust, laptop power mode and thermals all matter. A graphics card that benches low is often fixable, not finished — ask us before you replace it.

// DO YOU EVEN NEED ONE?

How much graphics power do you actually need?

The honest answer? Most people don’t need much. If your computer is for email, web, Microsoft 365, video calls and everyday tasks, even basic integrated graphics are plenty — and a slow computer is far more often about an old hard drive, too little memory or software clutter than the graphics card. Our full PC benchmark will show you where the real bottleneck is.

A powerful graphics card genuinely matters if you play modern games, edit 4K video, do 3D, CAD or rendering, run AI models locally, or drive several high-resolution screens. If that’s you and your score came back low, an upgrade transforms the experience.

Not sure which camp you’re in? That’s exactly what we’re here for — ask us and we’ll give you a straight answer, with no upsell.

// GOOD QUESTIONS

Frequently asked

How does this graphics benchmark work?

It renders a demanding real-time 3D scene in your browser using WebGL — the same technology games use — and measures how many frames per second your graphics card can push through it. From that it works out a graphics score, your average and 1% low frame rates, and roughly how your GPU compares. It’s all measured live on your own machine; nothing is installed or uploaded.

How accurate is it? Can I compare it to 3DMark?

Treat it as an indicative, relative guide rather than a lab-grade result. Browser, graphics drivers, laptop power mode, thermals and other open tabs all affect it, and every benchmark uses its own scale — so don’t compare the number directly with 3DMark, Unigine or Geekbench. Compare your machine against our tiers, or re-run it after a driver update or upgrade to see the change.

Is it safe? Will it harm my computer?

Completely safe. It simply makes your graphics card work hard for a few seconds — exactly like playing a game. The fan may spin up; that’s normal and stops the moment the test ends. Nothing is installed, downloaded or sent anywhere.

Why does it say my graphics card is ‘hidden’?

Some browsers, and stricter privacy settings, mask the exact graphics-chip name to reduce tracking. The benchmark still runs perfectly and your score is unaffected — we just can’t print the card’s name on your score card.

My score looks capped or lower than expected — why?

The most common reason is your screen’s refresh rate capping the frame rate (often 60 FPS) — run the Extreme test to push past it. On a laptop, plug it in and set Windows to Best Performance, because battery-saver throttles the GPU hard. Close other tabs and apps, and make sure hardware acceleration is switched on in your browser.

Does it work on laptops, Macs and phones?

Yes — any device with a modern browser. Laptops throttle on battery, so plug in for a fair result. Phones and tablets score much lower than desktops, which is completely normal — it’s a fun way to see the gap. We’re Windows specialists, so our advice is tuned with Windows PCs in mind.

What do the advanced options do?

Render scale changes the resolution the scene is drawn at (higher is harder on the GPU’s fill rate). Detail / quality controls how much work each pixel does. The effects — soft shadows, ambient occlusion, reflections and glow — each add realistic load, just like game settings. Turn everything up for a proper stress test.

My graphics card is too slow — what are my options?

It depends what you need it for. Sometimes the fix is free (updated drivers, better cooling, the right power mode); sometimes it’s a graphics-card upgrade or a new machine built around what you actually do. We’ll give you an honest steer — book a chat, try our custom PC builder, or see our workstation builds.

// HOW IT WORKS

How to benchmark your graphics card online

This free online GPU benchmark renders a demanding 3D scene in your browser using WebGL and measures how many frames your graphics card can push, giving a graphics score and a tier from integrated to enthusiast — no download and nothing installed.

Step by step

  1. Pick a test — Choose Quick, Standard or Extreme — Extreme pushes powerful cards past your screen’s frame-rate cap.
  2. Press Start — A live 3D scene renders while the tool measures your frame rate; you watch it run.
  3. Read your score and tier — You get a graphics score, average and 1% low FPS, and a tier from integrated to enthusiast.
  4. Try the advanced options — Change render scale, detail and effects (shadows, reflections) for a proper stress test, then save your score card.

Good to know

Is there an online GPU benchmark that needs no download?

Yes — this one runs entirely in your browser using WebGL, the same technology games use. It renders a real 3D scene, measures your frame rate, and gives a graphics score and tier, with nothing to install.

How accurate is a browser GPU benchmark?

Treat it as an indicative, relative guide rather than a lab result — browser, drivers, laptop power mode and thermals all affect it. Compare against our tiers or re-run after a driver update; don’t compare the number directly with 3DMark.
