The Dell OptiPlex 990 in 2026: still going — and honestly, that’s the point

The OptiPlex 990 was Dell’s top business desktop of 2011, and a remarkable number are still switched on every morning across Dorset — fifteen years later. Credit where it’s due

Still running after fifteen years? That’s exactly the point

The OptiPlex 990 launched in 2011 as the flagship of Dell’s business desktop range — 2nd-generation “Sandy Bridge” Intel Core i3, i5 and i7 processors, built for corporate fleets and sold in mini tower, desktop, small form factor and ultra small form factor cases. These machines were specified to survive years of office life, and plenty have survived a good deal more than that.

If yours still boots every morning, that isn’t luck. It’s business-grade build quality — and it’s precisely why we sell refurbished business-grade Dell rather than new budget machines. A consumer desktop from 2011 would almost certainly be in landfill by now. Your 990 is quietly making our case for us.

So none of what follows is a criticism of the machine. It’s simply what fifteen years does to any computer — and one deadline that’s now behind us.

The honest bit

Three real reasons it’s time to move on

1. The hard drive is living on borrowed time. Most 990s still have their original mechanical hard drive — a spinning metal platter that’s been running for well over a decade. Mechanical drives don’t last forever, and when one fails it often takes your photos, documents and accounts with it. If yours has never been replaced, the question isn’t if, it’s when.

2. The memory can’t keep up. Many 990s shipped with 4GB of DDR3 — generous in 2011, but a modern web browser with a handful of tabs can swallow that on its own. That’s the grinding slowness you’ve probably noticed.

3. The big one: no Windows 11 — and Windows 10 is finished. Windows 11 officially requires an 8th-generation Intel processor or newer, plus TPM 2.0. The 990’s 2nd-generation chips are six generations short, and it carries the older TPM 1.2. Microsoft ended Windows 10 support on 14 October 2025, so a 990 that goes online today no longer receives security updates — and every month that passes, the risk of using it for email, banking or shopping grows.

The bypass question

Can you force Windows 11 onto an OptiPlex 990? Yes — and we’d honestly advise against it

You may have seen registry tweaks and installer tricks that bypass the TPM and processor checks, and we’ll be straight with you: they do exist and they do work, in the sense that Windows 11 will install.

Here’s why we don’t recommend them:

  • Updates aren’t guaranteed. Microsoft states that unsupported installs may not receive updates — including security updates. You could end up exactly where you started: online without protection.
  • The hardware is still the hardware. A bypass doesn’t add memory, doesn’t make a 2011 processor faster, and doesn’t make a decade-old hard drive safer. Windows 11 on a 990 is the same struggling machine with a newer wallpaper.
  • You inherit the risk. If a future update refuses to install or breaks the machine, there’s no supported route out.

We’d rather tell you that plainly than sell you a workaround. For the fuller picture, see our guide to the “This PC can’t run Windows 11” message.

Honest triage

What we’d actually do with your OptiPlex 990

Not every 990 needs replacing, and we won’t pretend otherwise. Our upgrade services are available on request, and the first step is always an honest look at how the machine is used:

  • Offline or light duties? If your 990 runs a workshop label printer, drives an old bit of machinery, or lives entirely offline, an SSD and a memory top-up can genuinely give it a new lease of life — and we’ll happily quote for exactly that.
  • Online for email, browsing, banking or work? Then the Windows 10 deadline makes replacement the honest recommendation. No upgrade we could fit changes the fact that the machine can no longer run a supported operating system on the internet.

Tell us how you use it and we’ll tell you which side of that line yours falls on — over the phone, by text, or remotely. No call-out needed for the conversation, and no pressure either way. Call 01202 775566 or text 07520 615332.

Frequently asked

Can the Dell OptiPlex 990 run Windows 11?

Not officially. Windows 11 requires an 8th-generation Intel processor or newer plus TPM 2.0; the 990’s 2nd-generation “Sandy Bridge” chips are six generations short and it carries the older TPM 1.2. Registry bypasses exist, but Microsoft doesn’t guarantee updates on unsupported installs — so we honestly advise against them.

Is the OptiPlex 990 still good in 2026?

As a piece of engineering, genuinely yes — fifteen years of daily service proves the business-grade build. As an everyday internet computer, honestly no: Windows 10 support ended on 14 October 2025, so it can no longer run a supported operating system online. For offline or light duties, it can still earn its keep.

Is it safe to keep using my OptiPlex 990 on Windows 10?

Offline, yes. Online, the risk grows every month — Windows 10 stopped receiving security updates on 14 October 2025, so new vulnerabilities stay unpatched. We wouldn’t use one for email, banking or shopping, and we’d rather tell you that plainly. See our guide on Windows 10 ESU versus upgrading.

Should I just fit an SSD to my OptiPlex 990 instead?

Sometimes, honestly, yes. If the machine lives offline or does one light job, an SSD (and a memory top-up) transforms it and we’ll happily quote for exactly that — upgrade services are available on request. If it goes online for everyday use, an SSD doesn’t fix the Windows 10 problem, so replacement is the honest answer.

Its natural successors are the refurbished OptiPlex 3080 (guide £545) or OptiPlex 5080 (around £615) — the same business-grade family, eight processor generations newer.
