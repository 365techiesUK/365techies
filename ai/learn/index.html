/01 — START HERE: FOUR QUESTIONS WE HEAR MOST

Start here: four questions we hear most

If you’re wondering whether any of this applies to you — in your business or just at home, these four answers are the honest place to begin.



  • What can AI actually do for a small business?

    First, what it is: AI is software that can read and write everyday language — which is why a computer can now help with jobs it never could before. Today, reliably: draft and tidy written work, summarise long documents, answer repeat questions from your own information, and pull details out of emails and forms so nobody retypes them. Think of it as a capable assistant for well-defined steps — not a replacement for your judgement, and not something you switch on across the whole business at once.

  • What should never go into a chatbot?

    Passwords, card details and customers’ personal information have no place in a public AI tool — nor does anything commercially sensitive you wouldn’t email to an outside contractor. Free tools may use what you type to improve their models. Our Using AI Safely guide gives your team simple ground rules.

  • When is plain automation better than AI?

    When a task follows fixed rules — send the reminder, copy the record, raise the invoice — ordinary automation is faster, cheaper and never has an off day. AI earns its place only where a step needs reading, judgement or writing. Most systems we build are mostly straightforward automation, with AI on the one or two steps that genuinely need it — our automation service shows how that looks in practice.

  • How do I know if a process is worth automating?

    Three signs: it happens often, it follows a pattern you could explain to a new starter, and it eats time or causes mistakes when it’s rushed. If a job ticks all three, it’s a strong candidate; if it ticks none, leave it with a human. Our AI consultancy exists to work through exactly this question with you.

/02 — USING AI SAFELY

Using AI safely

The AI risks that actually bite small firms are ordinary ones: customer details pasted into a free tool, a confident answer that turned out to be wrong, an email sent without a person reading it first. None of them needs a security department to prevent — they need a few plain rules everyone in the business understands.

The Using AI Safely guide covers what to keep out of AI tools, how to check answers before you act on them, and where to keep a person in charge of the outcome. It’s the same approach we take in the systems we build: on anything consequential, a human approves before anything happens. Plain English throughout, no scare stories, and we keep it current as the tools change.

At home the risks look different — AI voice-cloning phone calls and convincing fake emails — and the free AI for Beginners course shows you how to spot both.

Frequently asked

Is the AI for Beginners course really free?

Yes — free means free. We built it because most owners want to understand AI before they spend anything on it, and an informed customer is easier to help well. If you take the course and never contact us, that’s a perfectly good outcome.

Do I need a technical background for any of this?

No. Everything here is written in plain English for people who run a business, and jargon is explained the first time it appears. If something still isn’t clear, that’s our fault rather than yours — tell us and we’ll rewrite it.

Is it safe for my staff to use tools like ChatGPT at work?

It can be, with ground rules: keep customer personal data, passwords and payment details out of public tools, and check important answers before acting on them. The Using AI Safely guide puts this in a form you can share with your team, and it’s a sensible first step before any wider AI plans.

Can you help us apply what we learn?

Yes. When reading turns into a specific idea, our AI consultancy works out whether a process is worth automating and what it would take, and our automation service builds and runs the result. Both start from the problem, not from a technology choice — you never need to pick a tool before talking to us.

Why is a local IT support company teaching AI?

Because customers kept asking, and because we already build and run AI-assisted systems in our own business every day. We’re a family-run Bournemouth firm, trading since 1995 with a 4.9-star Google rating, and explaining technology honestly has always been most of the job.
