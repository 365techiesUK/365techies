Welcome

// WHAT’S IN THIS COURSE

7 short lessons, at your own pace

Every lesson ends with one gentle “what would you do?” question — no scores, no time limits, no pass marks. Here’s what you’ll cover:

  1. Why shopping online is genuinely fine. Let’s start with the honest truth: shopping online is not a young person’s trick or a gamble.
  2. Choosing where to buy. The easiest rule in this whole course: start with names you already know.
  3. Paying wisely. How you pay matters just as much as where you buy.
  4. Your rights, in plain English. Here is something that surprises many people: when you shop online in the UK, your rights are generally stronger than in a physical shop.
  5. Buying from people, not shops. Websites such as Facebook Marketplace — and auction sites such as eBay when the seller is a private individual — are a different world from shopping with a business.
  6. Spotting shopping scams. Shopping scams almost never start with a shop — they start with a message .
  7. Keeping records: your paper trail. One last habit turns you from a hopeful shopper into a well-armed one: keep your paper trail.

// KEEP THIS BY THE COMPUTER

Your one-page checklist

The whole course, boiled down to the reminders that matter. Print it and keep it near the computer.

  1. Start with shops you know — and type the web address yourself rather than following links in emails or adverts.
  2. New shop? Search its name plus the word “reviews” before you spend a penny.
  3. A price that undercuts everyone else by half is a warning, not a bargain.
  4. The padlock means the connection is private — it does not mean the shop is honest.
  5. Over £100? Use a credit card — Section 75 makes the card company jointly responsible, up to £30,000.
  6. Never pay a shop or a stranger by bank transfer — it is like posting cash.
  7. Changed your mind? Most online purchases can go back within 14 days — and faulty goods rights last far longer.
  8. Buying from an individual on a marketplace? See it before you pay, collect locally, and never send money for something unseen.
  9. Delivery text asking for a small fee? Do not click — check your order yourself and forward the text to 7726 (free).
  10. Caught out? Ring your bank straight away — dialling 159 reaches most UK banks — report to Report Fraud (formerly Action Fraud) on 0300 123 2040 (in Scotland, ring Police Scotland on 101), and call us on 01202 775566 for friendly help.

// GOOD QUESTIONS

Frequently asked

Is this course really free?

Completely free, with no catch and no card details asked for. We are a family-run IT firm that has looked after Bournemouth homes and businesses since 1995, and this course is our way of helping local people shop online with confidence.

Is it actually safe to type my card details into a website?

At a reputable shop, yes — your card details are scrambled on their way across the internet, and the lessons show you how to judge which shops deserve them. Better still, paying by credit card or through a service such as PayPal adds real protection on top. The one thing we never recommend is paying a shop or a stranger by bank transfer.

What is Section 75, in plain English?

A British rule that makes your credit card company jointly responsible for items costing more than £100 and up to £30,000. If the goods never arrive, arrive faulty, or the shop vanishes, you can claim your money back from the card company itself. Lesson 3 walks through it gently.

I think I have already been caught out by a shopping scam. What should I do?

First: no shame whatsoever — these scams are professionally made and fool people of every age. If card or bank details were involved, ring your bank straight away; dialling 159 connects you safely to most UK banks. Forward scam texts to 7726 (free) and scam emails to report@phishing.gov.uk, and report it to Report Fraud (formerly Action Fraud) on 0300 123 2040 — in Scotland, ring Police Scotland on 101. And do call us on 01202 775566 if you would like a friendly hand with any of it.

Do I get a certificate?

Yes — a 365 Techies certificate of completion with your name on it, ready to print, plus a one-page checklist to keep by the computer. It is our own friendly certificate rather than an accredited qualification, but it marks something genuinely worth knowing.

Can someone show me all this in person?

Gladly. We run relaxed, in-person lessons at the Kinson Community Centre in Bournemouth — bring your own phone, tablet or laptop and your questions. Ring 01202 775566 and we will save you a seat.

Rather learn with a friendly human?

We run patient, plain-English computer lessons at the Kinson Community Centre — and we’re on the phone Mon–Fri, 9–5. No silly questions, ever.
