// REFURB VS CHEAP NEW

Business-grade refurb vs a cheap new consumer laptop

A common mistake is spending £400–£500 on a brand-new laptop from a supermarket shelf. Here’s the honest comparison, because the two are not the same thing:

  • A refurbished business-grade Latitude (from £510, new 1TB Samsung 990 PRO fitted) — a metal or reinforced chassis, a proper keyboard, usually upgradeable memory and storage, spare parts widely available, and engineering meant to survive years of daily use.
  • A £400–£500 brand-new consumer laptop — usually plastic, often with soldered-in memory and slow storage, built to a price and awkward to repair or upgrade later.

Nine times out of ten the refurbished business machine is faster, tougher and longer-lasting for similar money — and ours arrives with a new 1TB Samsung 990 PRO, set up and guaranteed. If you want to sanity-check that, read are refurbished laptops any good? and our refurbished vs new laptop guide — we’ll always give you the straight answer.

// GRADES & CHOOSING

Grades A, B and C — and choosing the right Dell

The grades are about looks, not health. Every machine is fully working and guaranteed — the grade just tells you the cosmetic condition:

  • Grade A — barely a mark; looks close to new.
  • Grade B — light, honest signs of use; usually the sweet spot for value.
  • Grade C — visibly used with scuffs, priced accordingly; works exactly as well.

Choosing the right Dell is mostly about matching the Latitude series to your work. As a rule of thumb the 3000 range is everyday budget, the 5000 range is the mainstream business workhorse, and the 7000 range is the premium ultralight. Our Latitude series explained guide walks through it, while are Dell Latitude laptops good? and how long they last cover the questions we hear most. Don’t worry about memorising specs — tell us what you do and we’ll match you.
