// DOMAIN & EMAIL RECOVERY

Business email stopped working after your domain expired?

When a domain lapses, the email and website that depend on it can go dark fast — inbound mail bounces and customers can’t reach you. Here’s exactly how to confirm the cause, renew or restore the domain, get your MX and DNS records right, and stop it ever happening again. Rated 4.9 on Google.

/01 — THE CAUSE

Why your business email stopped the moment the domain expired

Your email stopped because it depends on the domain being live. When a domain registration lapses, the domain stops resolving on the internet, so the MX records that tell the world where to deliver your mail disappear — and messages bounce instead of arriving. The fix is to renew the domain straight away, confirm your MX and DNS records are still correct, then allow a short propagation window for mail to start flowing again.

It usually hits all at once: incoming email bounces, outgoing email fails, and your website goes offline too, because the same domain underpins both. Some registrars park or suspend a domain the moment it expires, so everything can die on day one; others leave a short window before the domain is suspended. Either way, the clock is now ticking on how easily you can get it back.

The good news: if you act quickly, recovery is usually straightforward, and your original email address, mailbox contents and settings are untouched — renewing simply switches the domain back on. If you’d rather someone just handled it, our emergency IT help team can jump on it remotely, wherever you are in the UK.

/02 — CONFIRM IT

First, confirm the domain really is the problem

Before you change anything, make sure an expired domain is genuinely the cause and not a mailbox or password issue. The quickest check is a WHOIS lookup, which shows the registration status and expiry date. Run your domain through our free domain expiry checker — if it reports the registration as expired, suspended or in a grace period, you have your answer.

Next, check whether the domain still resolves and whether its mail records are present. Our DNS lookup tool (or a service like MXToolbox) will show whether any MX records are returned. If the lookup fails entirely or comes back empty, the domain isn’t live — consistent with expiry.

A few tell-tale signs it’s the domain and not something else:

  • Your website is down as well as email — both rely on the domain.
  • Senders receive bounce-backs saying the domain doesn’t exist or can’t be found.
  • Everyone on the domain is affected at once, not just one person.

If only one mailbox is affected, or your website is still fine, the cause is more likely a sign-in or sync problem — our email support pages cover those separately.

/03 — RECOVER THE DOMAIN

Renew the domain fast — and know your window

Recovery means renewing with your registrar (the company you bought the domain from). Log in, pay the outstanding renewal, and in most cases the domain switches back on. Speed matters, because the price and difficulty rise the longer you leave it — and the rules differ by domain type.

.co.uk and .uk domains (Nominet): you can renew at the standard price for up to 90 days after the expiry date. The domain is typically suspended around 30 days in, so email and the website stop, but it can still be reinstated by the original owner. After 90 days the domain enters cancellation and is then released for anyone to register.

.com, .net and .org domains: there’s usually a renewal grace period of around 30 days at the normal price. Miss that and the domain enters a redemption period — you can often still get it back, but the registrar charges a redemption or restore fee on top of the renewal. That fee varies widely between registrars and can run to a substantial sum, so renewing before it reaches redemption is far cheaper.

Exact timings and fees vary by registrar, so always check yours. If you’re not sure who your registrar is, or can’t get into the account, we can help you trace and recover it.

/04 — RESTORE EMAIL

Check your MX and DNS records, then let email flow

Renewing the domain switches it back on, but email won’t always spring back instantly. Two things need to be right: the domain has to start resolving again, and your mail records need to point to the correct mail provider.

If you simply renewed in time and nothing else was touched, your records usually survive intact and email returns on its own once DNS propagates — typically within a few hours, occasionally up to a day or so. If the domain had been deleted and restored, or the nameservers were reset, you may need to re-add your MX, SPF, DKIM and DMARC records so mail routes and authenticates properly again.

To verify everything is back, re-run our DNS lookup (or a tool like whatsmydns.net) and confirm your MX records match exactly what your email provider specifies. If you’re on Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, your provider publishes the precise records you should see.

Once the correct records are live and propagated, send a test message in and out. Don’t panic if the first few minutes are quiet — propagation isn’t instant, and it settles as the change spreads across the internet.

/05 — MAIL IN LIMBO

What happens to email sent while you were down

This is the honest part. While the domain was dead, anyone emailing you got a delivery failure, because there was nowhere to deliver to. What happens next depends on the sender’s mail server.

Some servers treat a missing domain as a temporary problem and quietly retry for a day or two, spacing attempts further apart, before giving up. If you renew quickly, a portion of that queued mail can still land once your records are live again. Other servers treat it as a permanent failure and bounce immediately — that mail is gone, and the sender would need to resend it.

There is no way to reach into the internet and pull back messages that already hard-bounced; they were never stored anywhere you can access. The practical steps are:

  • Get the domain live again as fast as possible to catch any mail still being retried.
  • Once email is flowing, message key clients and suppliers to say you had a brief outage and to resend anything from the affected dates.
  • Check whether any important senders got a bounce, so nothing time-critical slips through.

Acting fast genuinely reduces how much is lost.

/06 — PREVENTION

How to make sure it never happens again

Domain expiry is almost always avoidable. The single biggest cause we see is a renewal reminder that went to an email address on the very domain that expired — so the warning bounced along with everything else, and nobody saw it. Fix that first.

  • Turn on auto-renew at your registrar, and check the payment card on file hasn’t itself expired.
  • Use an off-domain contact address for your registrar account — a personal address, or one on a different domain — so reminders reach you even if the domain lapses.
  • Register for several years at once to cut down the number of renewal moments.
  • Keep your registrant, admin and billing contact details current, and make sure someone still at the business is named on them.
  • Know who your registrar is and keep the login safe — a surprising number of businesses have lost track of both.

It’s also worth diarising the renewal date independently, so you’re never relying on a single reminder. For the wider picture, our guide on protecting your business email covers the settings that keep mail secure and reliable, not just the domain.

// GOOD QUESTIONS

Frequently asked

How quickly will my email work again after I renew the domain?

Once the domain is renewed and live again, email returns as soon as DNS propagates — usually within a few hours, sometimes up to a day. If your MX records survived the lapse, mail flows on its own; if they were reset, they need re-adding first. Send a test message in and out to confirm, and don’t worry if the first few minutes are quiet.

My domain expired a few weeks ago — can I still get it back?

Very likely, yes. A .co.uk or .uk domain can be renewed at the standard price for up to 90 days after expiry. A .com, .net or .org usually has around a 30-day renewal grace period, after which it enters a redemption period where recovery costs extra. Act fast — the sooner you renew, the cheaper and simpler it is.

What if the domain has already been deleted or someone else has registered it?

Once a domain fully drops, it’s released for anyone to register, and if a third party has taken it we usually can’t force it back — that’s an honest limit worth knowing. If that’s happened, we’ll help you assess the options, and where recovery isn’t possible we’ll set you up on a new domain and migrate your email with as little disruption as we can.

Can I recover the emails people sent while my domain was down?

Partly, sometimes. Some sending servers retry for a day or two, so if you renew quickly a share of that mail still lands once you’re back. Anything that hard-bounced, though, is gone — it was never stored where you can reach it, and the sender would need to resend. The best move is to get the domain live fast, then ask key contacts to resend anything from the affected dates.

Will my website come back too, or just email?

Both come back. Your website and email rely on the same domain, so when it lapses they go down together and return together once the domain is renewed and DNS propagates. If your site sits on separate hosting, the pages and files are untouched — only the domain that points visitors to them went offline, and renewing restores that link.

Why didn’t I get a renewal reminder?

Usually because the reminder was sent to an email address on the domain that expired — so it bounced along with everything else. That’s the most common reason businesses get caught out. Set your registrar account to use an off-domain contact address, turn on auto-renew, and check the payment card on file is current so it can’t happen again.

My registrar is asking for a large redemption fee — is that normal?

For a .com, .net or .org that has passed its renewal grace period, yes — a redemption or restore fee on top of the renewal is standard, and it varies widely between registrars. .uk domains work differently and can usually be renewed at the standard price within 90 days. If you’re unsure whether a fee is fair or worth paying, call us and we’ll take a look.
