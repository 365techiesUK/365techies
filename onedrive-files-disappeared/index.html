// ONEDRIVE FILE RECOVERY

OneDrive files disappeared? Don't panic — they're usually recoverable

If your Documents, Desktop or Pictures have suddenly vanished on Windows 11, take a breath — in the vast majority of cases the files are still safe in OneDrive and can be brought back. This guide walks you through the safe way to find and restore them, without doing anything that risks overwriting your data. // FIRST, THE REASSURANCE

Your files are almost certainly still there

Nine times out of ten, "disappeared" OneDrive files are not deleted at all. They've usually been moved, paused, signed out, or shuffled into a folder you didn't expect — and OneDrive keeps a safety net besides. So before you do anything drastic, please don't reinstall Windows, don't run "recovery" software you found online, and don't keep creating new files in the same place. None of that helps, and some of it can genuinely make a real recovery harder.

OneDrive is Microsoft's cloud storage that syncs a copy of your files to the internet. That syncing is exactly why your files are so recoverable — even if something went wrong on this PC, a copy usually still lives safely in the cloud at onedrive.com, and OneDrive quietly keeps deleted items and older versions for a while too. The trick is knowing where to look. Work through the checks below in order; most people find their files within the first two or three steps. If you'd rather someone just did it for you, that's exactly the kind of thing we sort out remotely in a few minutes.

// THE MOST COMMON CULPRIT

Windows Backup may have moved your Desktop and Documents into OneDrive

This is the single most common reason people in Dorset ring us saying their files have "vanished". At some point — often after a Windows 11 update or when setting up a new PC — Windows offered to "back up your folders", and Known Folder Move quietly redirected your Desktop, Documents and Pictures into OneDrive. Nothing was lost. Your files simply live under OneDrive now instead of the old local path, so any shortcut or program still pointing at the old C:\Users\you\Documents location looks empty.

To check: open File Explorer and click OneDrive in the left-hand sidebar (it usually has a blue cloud icon). Look inside for Desktop, Documents and Pictures folders — if your missing files are in there, mystery solved. They're safe; they've just changed address. If you want to confirm it, right-click the folder that looks empty, choose Properties then the Location tab: if the path now starts with ...\OneDrive\, that tells you it's been redirected. We can tidy this up so everything opens where you expect, and explain in plain English whether to keep folder backup on (it's genuinely useful) or turn it off. If you'd like it set up properly, our Microsoft 365 support gets OneDrive and folder backup working the way you actually want.

// IS ONEDRIVE ACTUALLY RUNNING?

Check whether OneDrive is paused, signed out, or not syncing

Sometimes files look missing simply because OneDrive has stopped talking to the cloud. If it's paused, signed out, or hit a sync error, recent files may not have come down to this PC — or newer edits haven't gone up yet. Look for the little cloud icon in the bottom-right of your taskbar (you may need to click the small up-arrow to reveal hidden icons). Click it and check the status:

  • Greyed-out or "Signed out" — sign back in with the same Microsoft account. This matters: an old work account and a personal account show completely different files, so signing into the wrong one makes files look missing when they're safe under the other.
  • "Paused" — click the gear or the pause banner and choose Resume syncing. Give it a few minutes to catch up.
  • A red or yellow warning — there's a sync conflict or a full-storage problem. Don't force-delete anything; this is where a wrong move can bite. It's a good moment to let us take a look.

If OneDrive is missing from the taskbar entirely, search the Start menu for OneDrive and open it — don't uninstall it. And crucially, don't delete the local OneDrive folder to "reset" things; because it's linked to the cloud, deleting synced files there can remove them from the cloud too. When in doubt, stop and call us.

// THE SAFETY NETS

Recycle Bin, Files Restore — and checking your other devices

If the files really were deleted, OneDrive keeps safety nets on the website — and using them online is completely safe, because you're pulling copies back from the cloud, not overwriting anything on your PC.

1. The OneDrive Recycle Bin. Go to onedrive.com in your browser, sign in with your Microsoft account, and click Recycle bin in the left menu. For a personal Microsoft account, deleted OneDrive files stay here for 30 days (work or school accounts usually keep them for 93 days). Tick the ones you want and click Restore — they reappear in their original folder. This is different from the Recycle Bin on your Windows desktop, so always check the online one too — and note that files you deleted while they were "online-only" only show up in this web Recycle Bin, not the desktop one.

2. Files Restore ("Restore your OneDrive"). If lots of files vanished at once, or something scrambled a whole folder (a bad sync, a dodgy app, or ransomware), OneDrive can roll your entire OneDrive back to how it was at a chosen point in the last 30 days. On onedrive.com, open Settings (the gear, top-right), choose Options, then Restore your OneDrive, and pick a date from before things went wrong. One honest caveat: this feature is only available if you have a Microsoft 365 subscription (or a work/school account) — on a free OneDrive account you won't see it, and the Recycle Bin above is your main route. If it is available and you're unsure which point to pick, don't guess — we'll help you choose.

Two more quick wins. Search this PC properly — open File Explorer, click This PC, and type part of a filename (or an extension like *.docx) into the search box so Windows scans everywhere, not just one folder. And check another device signed into the same account — a phone, tablet, or onedrive.com in a browser. If the files show up there, they're safe and it's only this PC that's out of step. Been through it all and something important is still missing? Please stop before trying anything more aggressive — that's exactly when a calm pair of expert hands pays off, and our Dorset IT support team can take it from here.

// GOOD QUESTIONS

Frequently asked

Where do OneDrive files go when they disappear?

Usually one of four places: still in OneDrive but under a folder you didn't expect (often because Windows Backup moved your Desktop and Documents into OneDrive); not synced down because OneDrive is paused or signed out; sitting in the OneDrive Recycle Bin at onedrive.com if they were deleted; or safe on another device signed into the same account. They're genuinely deleted far less often than people fear — work through the checks on this page and most files turn up quickly.

How do I restore deleted files from OneDrive on Windows 11?

Go to onedrive.com in your browser and sign in with your Microsoft account, then click Recycle bin in the left menu, tick the files you want and click Restore — they'll reappear in their original folder. On a personal account, deleted items stay there for 30 days (93 days for most work or school accounts). If a lot of files vanished at once and you have a Microsoft 365 subscription, you can also use Settings then "Restore your OneDrive" to roll your whole OneDrive back to a point before things went wrong. Both work from the website and don't overwrite anything on your PC.

Why did my Documents folder suddenly empty on Windows 11?

Most often because Windows Backup (Known Folder Move) redirected your Documents, Desktop and Pictures into OneDrive — commonly after an update or new-PC setup. Nothing was deleted; the folder simply lives under OneDrive now, so anything still pointing at the old location looks empty. Open File Explorer, click OneDrive in the sidebar, and look for your Documents there. You can confirm it by right-clicking the empty-looking folder, choosing Properties then Location. We can re-point everything so it opens where you expect.

I turned off OneDrive and my files vanished — are they gone?

Almost certainly not, but please stop and don't delete anything. When you unlink or turn off OneDrive, the online copies stay safe in the cloud — the local folder can just look empty or out of date. The real risk is deleting synced files from the local OneDrive folder to "tidy up", because that can remove them from the cloud too. Sign back into onedrive.com to confirm your files are there, and let us re-link OneDrive properly so everything comes back cleanly.

We connect with your permission using Splashtop SOS (we phone you first, you watch the whole session on screen, and it ends the moment we're done), find where your files actually went, and restore them safely from OneDrive's recycle bin or Files Restore. Will trying to recover the files make things worse?

The safe steps on this page won't — searching your PC and restoring copies from onedrive.com only ever brings files back, never overwrites them. What can make things worse is reinstalling Windows, running unknown "recovery" tools, deleting synced files from the local OneDrive folder, or signing out and back in with the wrong account. If you've tried the safe checks and something's still missing, stop there and give us a ring rather than risk it.
