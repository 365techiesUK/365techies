// THE QUESTION BEHIND MOST SPEC CHECKS

Can this PC run Windows 11?

A browser cannot read the two things that decide it, so this page will not pretend to. Here is what Microsoft requires, and where Windows itself tells you.

  • The minimums

    A compatible 64-bit processor — roughly Intel 8th generation or AMD Ryzen 2000 onwards — 4 GB of memory, 64 GB of storage, a DirectX 12 graphics chip and a 720p screen.

  • The two deciders

    TPM 2.0 and Secure Boot. Both live in the firmware, not the browser. On Windows: Settings → Privacy & security → Windows Security → Device security lists them as Security processor and Secure boot.

  • Still on Windows 10?

    Free security updates ended in October 2025. Whatever the check says there is a route: an upgrade if it passes, a refurbished business machine if it does not, or Extended Security Updates while you decide — the three below, or the full Windows 10 page.

// GOOD QUESTIONS

Frequently asked

Is any of this information sent to you?

No — none of it. Everything on this page is read by your own browser, shown only to you, and vanishes when you close the tab. The only way we ever see your specs is if you download the sheet and choose to send it to us.

How much RAM do I actually have?

This page shows the rounded figure your browser is allowed to share, and browsers cap that at 8 GB even on a machine with far more. For the exact amount on Windows, press Ctrl+Shift+Esc for Task Manager, choose Performance, then Memory. Or install the free 365 PC Manager, which shows the amount fitted and how much is in use.

Can this page tell me whether my PC can run Windows 11?

Not on its own — a browser cannot read the two things that decide it, TPM 2.0 and Secure Boot. Windows can: open Settings → Privacy & security → Windows Security → Device security, where they appear as Security processor and Secure boot. Our Windows 11 upgrade service checks it for free and does the upgrade properly if it passes; if it falls short, a refurbished business Dell from £510 with your data moved across is the sensible replacement, and Windows 10 ESU keeps it patched until October 2027 while you decide.

Is it safe to run the benchmark on this page?

Yes. It runs for about twenty seconds inside your browser using ordinary web features, works the processor, memory, graphics and storage hard for a moment, and installs nothing. The fan may spin up briefly, which is normal. Your score stays on your device unless you choose to download the score card or share it.

Why can’t it show my exact RAM, CPU model or temperatures?

Because browsers deliberately hide them — and that’s good privacy design, since the same details could be used to track you. Most browsers share a rounded memory figure, a core count and usually the graphics card name — some hide even those — but never serial numbers, temperatures, fan speeds or your exact CPU model. For the engineer-grade readout we check all of that on a service.

How do I find the full detail myself on Windows?

Press Start and type About your PC for the basics (Windows edition, processor, RAM), or type dxdiag and press Enter for the fuller system report including your graphics card. Stuck reading it? Send us the spec sheet from this page and we’ll translate.

It says I’m on Windows 10 — is that bad?

It needs attention: Windows 10 stopped receiving free security updates in October 2025, so it gets riskier to use online every month. Many machines can move to Windows 11 free — see your options or call us and we’ll check yours in minutes remotely.

What does ‘graphics running in software’ mean?

It means your real graphics card isn’t being used, so the processor is drawing everything the hard way — video calls stutter, video playback eats the battery and everything feels sluggish. It’s usually a driver or settings problem and is very fixable, often remotely.

How accurate is the refresh-rate reading?

It’s measured live by timing how fast your screen actually draws frames, so it reflects your current setting — laptops sometimes drop to 60 Hz on battery even when the screen can do more. If it reads lower than your screen’s rating, check your power mode and display settings.

Specs raising questions?

Windows 10 warning, mystery slowness, or a machine that doesn’t match what you thought you bought — send us your spec sheet and we’ll give you an honest answer, usually within minutes remotely.

How to check your computer’s specs online

This free checker instantly reveals what your browser can see about your computer — your operating system, graphics card, screen resolution, memory and processor cores — with nothing installed, though a browser can’t read every internal detail like the exact CPU model.

Step by step

  1. Open the checker — It reads what your browser exposes about your device automatically — no permissions or install needed.
  2. Read your specs — See your operating system, graphics card, screen resolution and colour depth, memory hint and processor core count.
  3. Save the spec sheet — Download or copy the summary — handy when buying software, asking for support or selling a device.
  4. Need the full detail? — For the exact CPU, RAM and drive models a browser can’t see, our PC Hardware Checker guidance and our team can help.

Good to know

How do I check my computer’s specs online?

Open this checker and it instantly shows what your browser can detect — operating system, graphics card, screen resolution, an estimate of memory and your processor’s core count — with nothing installed and nothing sent to us.

Why can’t the checker see my exact CPU or RAM?

Browsers deliberately limit what a website can read about your hardware for privacy, so some details (like the precise processor model or total RAM) aren’t available. What it does show is accurate; for the full picture, Windows’ own System Information or our team can help.
