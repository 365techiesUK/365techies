// RECOVERING TREASURED PHOTOS

Getting the photos off a deceased relative’s laptop

Losing someone is hard enough without a locked laptop standing between you and the photos you want to keep. In almost every case those pictures and files can be recovered gently and privately — even without the password — and we can take the whole job off your hands whenever you feel ready. /01 — THE SHORT ANSWER

Can you get photos off a deceased relative’s laptop?

In almost every case, yes — the photos, videos and documents can be recovered, even without the password. The pictures you’re hoping to find don’t live inside the Windows login; they sit on the laptop’s hard drive or SSD as ordinary files, and that drive can usually be read directly on another computer without ever signing in.

In practical terms, the storage drive is carefully removed from the laptop (or the laptop is started using a separate tool) and connected to a second machine, where its contents appear like any other folder. From there the family photos in the Pictures and Documents folders — and anything saved to the desktop or downloads — can be copied off to a USB stick, an external drive or a private cloud folder for you to keep.

There’s no need to rush any of this. If the laptop is sitting in a drawer, the photos on it are safe for now, and they’ll still be there whenever you feel ready. When you are, we can take the whole job off your hands gently and privately — more on exactly how below. The two things that occasionally get in the way are full-disk encryption and a physically failed drive, and we’ll be honest with you about both.

/02 — WHY YOU RARELY NEED THE PASSWORD

Why the password usually isn’t a barrier

A Windows password protects the login screen, not the files underneath it. When a drive is connected to another computer as a second, non-booting disk, Windows doesn’t ask for that password at all — it simply reads the drive like a large USB stick.

The photos are almost always in a predictable place. On a Windows laptop, each person’s files sit under their own user folder, in sub-folders such as Pictures, Documents, Desktop and Downloads. Photos copied across from a phone or camera often end up here too. Occasionally the drive will show an “access denied” message because of file permissions; that’s a routine hurdle a technician clears by taking ownership of the folders, and it doesn’t mean the files are lost.

It’s worth knowing that many people’s recent photos are also quietly backed up to the cloud — to OneDrive, Google Photos or similar — without them ever realising. If your relative used any of these, some of the pictures may already be recoverable from their online account as well as from the laptop. We can help you check both, so nothing precious is missed. If you’d like to understand how photo backups work, our guide on how to back up your photos explains it in plain English.

/03 — WHEN THE LAPTOP WON’T SWITCH ON

If the laptop won’t turn on

A laptop that won’t turn on is worrying, but it very rarely means the photos are gone. A machine can fail to start for dozens of reasons — a dead battery or charger, a failed screen, a corrupted Windows installation — while the storage drive inside stays perfectly healthy. In most of these cases the drive can be removed and read exactly as described above, and the pictures come off intact.

There is one important exception to watch for. If the laptop makes a repeated clicking, grinding or beeping noise, or was dropped or exposed to liquid, the drive itself may be physically damaged. If that’s the case, the single most helpful thing you can do is stop using it and switch it off — repeatedly powering a failing drive on and off can turn a recoverable situation into an unrecoverable one. Set it aside and let someone look at it before trying anything else.

If your relative’s laptop shows no signs of life at all, our guide on a laptop that won’t turn on with no lights walks through the harmless checks worth trying first. And if it still won’t start, please don’t worry — a dead laptop and a perfectly readable drive very often go together.

/04 — THE ONE THING THAT CAN STOP US

The honest exception: encryption

The honest exception is full-disk encryption. Some Windows laptops — particularly newer or business machines — have BitLocker encryption switched on, which scrambles everything on the drive so it can’t be read without a recovery key. If that’s the case, removing the drive isn’t enough on its own; the key is needed to unlock it.

The good news is that the recovery key is usually findable. It may have been:

  • saved to your relative’s Microsoft account online (the most common place), if they signed in to Windows with an email address;
  • printed or written down and kept with their computer paperwork;
  • saved to a USB stick when encryption was first switched on.

We’ll happily help you look in all the likely places. What we won’t do is give you false hope: Microsoft cannot recreate a lost BitLocker key, and if a drive is encrypted and the key genuinely can’t be found, the files on it may not be recoverable by any means. Many older home laptops aren’t encrypted at all, though newer Windows 11 machines increasingly have it switched on by default, so we’d far rather tell you honestly up front than have you spend money chasing something that isn’t there. We’ll always check whether encryption is in the way before we quote.

/05 — WHAT WE RECOVER & HOW YOU GET IT BACK

What we recover, and how you get it back

The aim is simple: to get everything that matters off the machine and into your hands, in a form you can open and keep. That normally includes photos and videos, scanned pictures, documents and letters, and often things people forget about — saved emails, a contacts list, address books, and files tucked away on the desktop.

Once the files are gathered, we copy them onto whatever suits you best: a USB memory stick or external drive you can hold onto, or a private cloud folder you can share with the rest of the family so everyone has their own copy. We can also help you get the photos onto phones and tablets so they’re always close to hand.

Crucially, the original laptop and its drive are left untouched until you’re happy — we work from the recovered copy, so nothing is deleted from the source in the process. If you later want the pictures organised, duplicates cleared out, or a proper backup set up so they can never be lost again, we can do that too. Our page on data recovery explains the wider service, and moving files safely covers getting everything onto a new device.

/06 — HOW WE CAN HELP

Handing it over — gently and privately

This is a job we handle often, and we know it usually comes at a hard time. You don’t have to be technical, and you don’t have to do any of it yourself. If you’d rather simply hand it over, here’s how it works with us:

  • Take as long as you need. There’s no pressure and no deadline — get in touch whenever you feel ready, whether that’s next week or next year.
  • Collection or a home visit. We can collect the laptop from you across Bournemouth, Poole, Christchurch and the wider Dorset area, or help remotely if you prefer — whatever is easiest for you.
  • Handled privately. Everything on the machine is treated as private and confidential. We recover only what you’ve asked for, and nothing on the drive is shared with anyone.
  • No fix, no fee. We’ll always give you a clear, no-obligation quote first, and if we genuinely can’t recover anything, you won’t be charged for the recovery.

As a family-run business in Kinson, Bournemouth — helping people here since 1995 — we’ll talk to you like a person, not a ticket number. If you’d like to start, you can get in touch or call us on 01202 775566, and we’ll take it from there, gently.

/07 — KEEPING THE MEMORIES SAFE

Afterwards: keeping the memories safe

Once the photos and files are safely recovered, there are usually two loose ends to tidy — and, again, there’s no rush on either.

The first is protecting the copies so they can never be lost again. A single USB stick can fail or be misplaced, so we’d gently suggest keeping the recovered photos in at least two places — for example, one physical copy and one in the cloud. We’re happy to set that up so the whole family has access.

The second is the laptop itself. Some families want to keep it exactly as it is; others would like it wiped so it can be passed on, donated or recycled without any personal information left behind. When you’re ready, we can securely erase the drive so nothing of your relative’s remains, and dispose of the machine responsibly if you don’t wish to keep it. Our guides on wiping and recycling an old computer and secure IT disposal explain how this is done properly.

Normally it’s the executor or next of kin who arranges this sort of thing, but that’s a decision for your family — our part is purely the practical work of recovering the files and, if you wish, clearing the machine afterwards.

// GOOD QUESTIONS

Frequently asked

Can you get the photos off without the password?

Yes, in almost all cases. Your relative’s photos are stored as files on the laptop’s drive, separate from the Windows login. By reading that drive directly on another computer, we can reach the Pictures, Documents and desktop folders without ever needing the password. The only common exception is a drive protected by encryption, which needs a recovery key.

The laptop won’t turn on at all. Are the photos lost?

Usually not. A laptop can fail to start because of a dead battery, a broken screen or a damaged Windows installation while the storage drive inside stays perfectly healthy — and the photos can still be recovered from that drive. The exception is a drive making a clicking or grinding noise, which suggests physical damage; if you hear that, switch the laptop off and let us look before trying anything else.

Is there anything that would stop you recovering the files?

Two things, and we’ll always be straight with you about them. If the drive is protected by BitLocker encryption and the recovery key genuinely can’t be found, the files may be unreadable by anyone, including us. And if the drive has suffered serious physical failure, recovery becomes specialist work and isn’t always possible. We check for both before quoting — encryption is more common on newer Windows 11 laptops than it used to be, but the recovery key can usually be found, and genuine physical failure is uncommon.

Can you just do all of this for me?

Yes — that’s exactly what most families ask for, and you don’t need to be technical. We can collect the laptop from across Bournemouth, Poole, Christchurch and Dorset, recover the photos and files, and hand them back to you on a USB stick or in a private cloud folder. You’re welcome to take as long as you need before starting, and it’s no fix, no fee.

Do I need to prove I’m allowed to access the laptop?

We don’t give legal advice, but as a rule it’s the executor or next of kin who arranges recovering a loved one’s files, and that’s a decision for your family to make. Our role is simply the practical, technical work — getting the photos and documents off the machine and, if you wish, wiping it afterwards — carried out privately and with care.

How will I actually get the photos back?

Whatever suits you best. We usually copy everything onto a USB memory stick or external drive you can keep, or into a private cloud folder you can share with the rest of the family so everyone has their own copy. We can also help load the photos onto a phone or tablet. The original laptop is left untouched until you’re completely happy.

What does it cost?

Because every machine is different, we give you a clear, no-obligation quote first — there are no surprises. Our recovery work is no fix, no fee, so if we genuinely can’t recover anything, you won’t pay for the recovery. Just call us on 01202 775566 or get in touch and we’ll talk it through with you.

Can you wipe the laptop afterwards so we can pass it on?

Yes, whenever you’re ready. Once you have the recovered photos safely, we can securely erase the drive so none of your relative’s personal information is left behind, and help you donate or recycle the machine responsibly if you don’t want to keep it. There’s no rush — many families keep the laptop for a while first. Our secure IT disposal page explains more.

Will you look through the photos and files?

No. Everything on the machine is treated as private and confidential. We recover the folders you’ve asked for and hand them straight back to you — we don’t browse, copy or share anything else on the drive. If it would put your mind at ease, we’re glad to talk through exactly what will and won’t be touched before we begin.
