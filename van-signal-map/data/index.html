    The method

    • A Netgear Nighthawk M5 router in the van, on a Three UK SIM, reporting signal strength, quality (SINR), band and network type.
    • Home Assistant records a reading every 30 seconds while the van is out, and runs a full download speed test every 5 minutes.
    • Each reading is pinned to where it was taken. A speed test only counts towards a place if it ran within about seven minutes of that reading.
    • Places are grouped into roughly 220-metre cells and reported as a median, so one lucky or unlucky test can’t set the figure.

    The limits, plainly

    • One van, one network. Three UK only. Another network at the same spot may be better or worse, and we have not tested it.
    • A few readings were taken on foot — a phone on the same network, running the same 10 MB test, in places the van can’t reach, such as the beach. They’re drawn as small diamonds, marked “on foot”, and are never pooled into the ranked places or the published figures.
    • One vehicle’s routes. This is where our support van actually goes — not a survey grid. Coverage is uneven by construction.
    • A moment in time. Mobile speed varies with congestion, weather and the mast’s own load. A spot measured once at 09:00 may differ at 18:00.
    • Signal strength is not speed. We publish SINR because a strong-looking signal full of interference is slow — see the explanation above the map.
    • Not a coverage map. Ofcom and the networks publish modelled coverage for the whole country. We publish a small number of real measurements. Those are different things, and theirs is broader.
