    Readings appear after a short delay — the map shows where the van has been, never where it is right now. Best spots ranks the places our own speed tests measured fastest — how we choose where to park and work.

    Why “full bars” can still be slow

    Every reading here carries SINR — signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio. Signal strength only tells you how loud the mast is; SINR tells you how much of that is actually usable rather than drowned in interference from other traffic. It’s why a spot showing four bars can crawl while a weaker-looking one flies: our own van has measured 4 bars at 2–12 Mbps parked in one place and 75 Mbps in another. Click any point to see its SINR — roughly 20+ excellent, 13+ good, 5+ fair, below that poor.

    Being straight about the numbers: SINR is an instant snapshot and it moves constantly while driving — ours swung between −6 and 26 dB on a single trip — whereas a speed test averages over the seconds it runs. So an individual point can pair a poor SINR with a good speed, or the reverse. The relationship is real, but it shows up properly when the van is parked, which is exactly the case that matters if you’re choosing somewhere to work.

    / HOW IT WORKS

    Measured, not modelled

    • The van measures

      A router in the campervan reads the live 4G/5G signal — strength, quality, band, network — and Home Assistant logs it every 30 seconds while driving, running a full download speed test every five minutes.

    • Tagged to the spot

      Each reading is pinned to exactly where it was taken, so you see the real signal at a real place — not a coverage prediction averaged over a grid square.

    • Ranked, not traced

      Each reading lands on the map as its own point — green where it was fast, red where it wasn’t — and the places that tested best are ranked for you. We plot the measurements, not the journey.

    Built by 365 Techies with Home Assistant and a 5G router — the same off-grid monitoring we build for campervans, boats and remote sites.
