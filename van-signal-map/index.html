    Why “full bars” can still be slow

    Every reading here carries SINR — signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio. Signal strength only tells you how loud the mast is; SINR tells you how much of that is actually usable rather than drowned in interference from other traffic. It’s why a spot showing four bars can crawl while a weaker-looking one flies: our own van has measured 4 bars at 2–12 Mbps parked in one place and 75 Mbps in another. Click any point to see its SINR — roughly 20+ excellent, 13+ good, 5+ fair, below that poor.

    Being straight about the numbers: SINR is an instant snapshot and it moves constantly while driving — ours swung between −6 and 26 dB on a single trip — whereas a speed test averages over the seconds it runs. So an individual point can pair a poor SINR with a good speed, or the reverse. The relationship is real, but it shows up properly when the van is parked, which is exactly the case that matters if you’re choosing somewhere to work.

    / METHOD & LIMITS

    How we measured it — and what it can’t tell you

    The method

    • A Netgear Nighthawk M5 router in the van, on a Three UK SIM, reporting signal strength, quality (SINR), band and network type.
    • Home Assistant records a reading every 30 seconds while the van is out, and runs a full download speed test every 5 minutes.
    • Each reading is pinned to where it was taken. A speed test only counts towards a place if it ran within about seven minutes of that reading.
    • Places are grouped into roughly 220-metre cells and reported as a median, so one lucky or unlucky test can’t set the figure.

    The limits, plainly

    • One van, one network. Three UK only. Another network at the same spot may be better or worse, and we have not tested it.
    • A few readings were taken on foot — a phone on the same network, running the same 10 MB test, in places the van can’t reach, such as the beach. They’re drawn as small diamonds, marked “on foot”, and are never pooled into the ranked places or the published figures.
    • One vehicle’s routes. This is where our support van actually goes — not a survey grid. Coverage is uneven by construction.
    • A moment in time. Mobile speed varies with congestion, weather and the mast’s own load. A spot measured once at 09:00 may differ at 18:00.
    • Signal strength is not speed. We publish SINR because a strong-looking signal full of interference is slow — see the explanation above the map.
    • Not a coverage map. Ofcom and the networks publish modelled coverage for the whole country. We publish a small number of real measurements. Those are different things, and theirs is broader.

    Credit where it’s due: Dorset Council and Streetwave have measured mobile coverage across the Dorset Council area using their refuse fleet — a far larger survey than ours. Their work does not cover the Bournemouth, Christchurch and Poole council area, which is where our van mostly drives.

    / HOW IT WORKS

    Measured, not modelled

    • The van measures

      A router in the campervan reads the live 4G/5G signal — strength, quality, band, network — and Home Assistant logs it every 30 seconds while driving, running a full download speed test every five minutes.

    • Tagged to the spot

      Each reading is pinned to exactly where it was taken, so you see the real signal at a real place — not a coverage prediction averaged over a grid square.

    • Ranked, not traced

      Each reading lands on the map as its own point — green where it was fast, red where it wasn’t — and the places that tested best are ranked for you. We plot the measurements, not the journey.

    Built by 365 Techies with Home Assistant and a 5G router — the same off-grid monitoring we build for campervans, boats and remote sites.
