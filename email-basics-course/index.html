Email, without the worry

A free, friendly course from the family-run firm that has been untangling technology in Bournemouth since 1995. Seven short lessons take the worry out of email — how it works, replying without fear, attachments, and keeping your account safe — all at your own pace, with a printable certificate at the end. No jargon, nothing to install, nothing to pay.

Welcome

// WHAT’S IN THIS COURSE

7 short lessons, at your own pace

Every lesson ends with one gentle “what would you do?” question — no scores, no time limits, no pass marks. Here’s what you’ll cover:

  1. How email actually works. Let’s begin with what email really is, because once you picture it clearly the rest feels easy.
  2. Reading and replying without fear. Opening an email to read it is as safe as opening a letter — simply select it from the list and it opens for you to read.
  3. Sending well: subjects, To, CC and BCC. Now for sending your own emails, which is where a couple of small habits make you look like an old hand.
  4. Attachments, both ways. An attachment is a file clipped to an email — a photo, perhaps, or a document.
  5. Taming the inbox. As the emails pile up, two buttons keep the clutter down.
  6. Email safety essentials. This lesson ties everything to one big idea, and it is worth saying plainly: your email account is the key to everything else .
  7. A little housekeeping. Congratulations — the essentials are behind you, and this last lesson is a gentle extra: a few “housekeeping” touches that make email feel like your own comfortable armchair.

// KEEP THIS BY THE COMPUTER

Your one-page checklist

The whole course, boiled down to the reminders that matter. Print it and keep it near the computer.

  1. Picture your email address as your own postbox online - with webmail you can read the same inbox on a laptop, tablet or phone.
  2. Use Reply to answer only the sender; use Reply all only when you truly mean everyone on the message to see it.
  3. Give every email a short, clear subject line so the reader knows what it is about before opening.
  4. Emailing a group who do not all know each other? Put their addresses in BCC so each address stays hidden from the rest.
  5. Only open an attachment when you know the sender and were expecting it - if in doubt, leave it and check first.
  6. Delete junk; archive things you may want later; and glance in your Junk or Spam folder now and then for genuine mail.
  7. Only click Unsubscribe on emails from companies you signed up to - never on suspicious ones, where you should delete instead.
  8. Give your email account your strongest password and use it nowhere else - it is the key to your other accounts.
  9. Scams rush you or scare you, so slow down: no genuine bank or company will email asking for your password or a code, or tell you to move money to a safe account.
  10. If a scam strikes: forward scam texts to 7726, forward dodgy emails to report@phishing.gov.uk, and call your bank - 159 puts you through to most banks. Stuck? Call 365 Techies on 01202 775566.

// GOOD QUESTIONS

Frequently asked

Is this course really free?

Completely free, with no catch and no card details asked for. We are a family-run IT firm that has looked after Bournemouth homes and businesses since 1995, and this course is our way of helping local people feel confident with everyday technology.

Do I need to install anything or create an account?

Nothing at all for the course itself — it runs right here in your web browser. To send and receive email you will need an email account, and the big free ones such as Gmail and Outlook.com are easy to set up when you feel ready. If your internet provider gave you an address ending in the provider’s name — @btinternet.com, for example — that works perfectly well too.

Which should I use — Gmail or Outlook.com?

Honestly, either is excellent, and they are more alike than different. Gmail comes from Google and Outlook.com from Microsoft; both are free and both keep your email safely online. Our gentle advice is to pick one, get comfortable, and not worry that you have chosen the “wrong” one — you have not. A provider address such as @btinternet.com is fine to keep using as well — though it is worth knowing that if you ever switch internet provider, some providers close the address that came with the account, which is one reason many people like a free address that stays with them for good.

Is the certificate a qualification?

It is a 365 Techies certificate of completion — a lovely thing to print, keep or show the grandchildren, but not an accredited qualification. The real prize is the confidence and the safe habits you take away with you.

I have never sent an email before. Is this too advanced for me?

Quite the opposite — it was written especially for complete beginners. Every idea is explained in plain English, every lesson is short, and there is no timer, no pass mark and no way to fail. You set the pace, and you can revisit any lesson as often as you like.

Can I get help in person?

Absolutely. We run relaxed, friendly in-person lessons at the Kinson Community Centre in Bournemouth — no question too small. Call us on 01202 775566 and we will find a time that suits you.

Rather learn with a friendly human?

We run patient, plain-English computer lessons at the Kinson Community Centre — and we’re on the phone Mon–Fri, 9–5. No silly questions, ever.
