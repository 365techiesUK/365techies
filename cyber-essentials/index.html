// THE APRIL 2026 RULES, IN PLAIN ENGLISH

What Cyber Essentials actually asks for

Cyber Essentials is the UK government’s basic security certificate, run by the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) with IASME. Since 27 April 2026 it’s assessed against the NCSC’s Requirements for IT Infrastructure v3.3. It covers five areas, on every computer, laptop, phone, tablet, server, router and cloud service your business uses for work.

1

Firewalls

A firewall on between every device and the internet: the router’s, plus Windows Firewall on laptops that leave the office. The router’s admin password changed from the one it came with, and nothing opened to the internet without a reason.

2

Secure set-up

No spare or default accounts left on, no software you don’t need, auto-run off, and a lock after 10 wrong guesses or fewer. Passwords of at least 12 characters (or 8 with two-step sign-in); a PIN that only unlocks the device can be 6.

3

Security updates — 14 days

Every critical or high-risk fix installed within 14 days of release, for Windows, your programs, your router and your computers’ firmware (BIOS). Automatic updates on wherever possible, and anything that no longer gets fixes removed. Missing this fails the assessment.

4

Accounts & two-step sign-in

Everyday work in an everyday account; administrator accounts only for installing and setting up — never for email or browsing. Two-step sign-in (MFA) on every cloud service that offers it, Microsoft 365 and Google included. Since April 2026, missing it is an automatic fail.

5

Malware protection

Antivirus on every computer, kept up to date as its maker recommends (Microsoft Defender counts), and harmful websites blocked. On phones and tablets, apps only from the official stores.

// THE ONE THAT CATCHES PEOPLE OUT

Microsoft releases Windows’ security fixes on the second Tuesday of every month. Under Cyber Essentials they need to be on every PC within 14 days — every month, not just on assessment day.

That’s why passing once isn’t the hard part: staying ready all year is. An assessor checking for Cyber Essentials Plus looks for fixes older than 14 days.

// GOOD QUESTIONS

Frequently asked

What is Cyber Essentials?

A UK government-backed certificate, run by the National Cyber Security Centre with IASME, showing an organisation has five basic security controls in place: firewalls, secure set-up, security updates, account control and malware protection. Many contracts and insurers ask for it.

What changed in April 2026?

Assessments from 27 April 2026 use the NCSC’s Requirements for IT Infrastructure v3.3 (the “Danzell” question set). Two-step sign-in (MFA) on cloud services is now an automatic fail if it’s missing, and the 14-day rule for critical and high-risk updates covers firmware as well as Windows and programs.

Do updates really have to go in within 14 days?

Yes, for anything rated critical or high-risk (or not rated at all): Windows, your programs, your router and your computers’ firmware. Windows’ fixes come out on the second Tuesday of the month, so each month’s are due two weeks later. Installing every update within 14 days is recommended rather than required.

How much does Cyber Essentials cost?

IASME’s assessment fee is £320 plus VAT for 0 to 9 staff, £440 for 10 to 49, £500 for 50 to 249 and £600 for 250 or more (October 2026). Getting ready is quoted before we start; on our business plans, keeping you ready all year is included.

How long does the certificate last?

12 months. You renew it every year, and the requirements are updated most years, usually in April.

Can 365 PC Manager make my business compliant?

No app can, and it never claims to. It checks what one Windows PC can show and tells you what to fix. Your router, cloud accounts, phones and policies need checking too, and only an IASME certification body can award the certificate.

Is the readiness check really free?

Yes. It’s part of 365 PC Manager, free for any Windows 10 or 11 PC, with no sign-up. The year-round watch, reminders and dated record are free on that PC too; Cyber Essentials Ready across all your computers comes with our business plans.

Do you certify businesses yourselves?

No. We prepare you, fix what’s needed and help with the question set; an independent IASME certification body marks the assessment. That independence is part of what makes the certificate worth having.

What about Cyber Essentials Plus?

Plus adds a hands-on check by the assessor: they test a sample of your devices, including looking for missing updates more than 14 days old. Staying ready all year is what makes Plus straightforward.

Do you cover businesses outside Bournemouth?

Yes. We look after businesses across Bournemouth, Christchurch, Poole and Dorset, and most of the work is done remotely, so we can help anywhere in the UK.
