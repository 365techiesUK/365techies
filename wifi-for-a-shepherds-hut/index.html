How to get reliable Wi-Fi to a shepherd’s hut

The best way to get Wi-Fi to a shepherd’s hut comes down to one thing: whether you can see a building that already has broadband from where the hut stands. If you can, a point-to-point wireless link is usually the fastest and best-value fix; if you can’t, a 4G or 5G router with a proper external antenna, or Starlink for truly remote spots, will do the job. We survey your site across Dorset and the New Forest, recommend honestly, and can even power the whole thing off-grid with solar and lithium. Rated 4.9 on Google.

// THREE WAYS IN

Three honest ways to get internet to a hut

The right answer depends almost entirely on one question: can you see a building that already has broadband from where the hut stands? Walk it through with these three options and you’ll usually know which way to go before we even arrive.

  • You can see the main house — point-to-point wireless. A small pair of antennas beams the existing broadband across your land. Usually the fastest and best-value fix.
  • No line-of-sight, but there’s mobile signal — a 4G or 5G router. A router with a proper external antenna pulls in a mobile signal and shares it as Wi-Fi.
  • No signal and no line-of-sight — Starlink. Satellite broadband for genuinely remote huts where nothing else reaches.

Inside the hut we add a router or a small mesh point so the Wi-Fi actually reaches the bed, the sofa and the deck — not just the wall where the aerial lands.

// LINE-OF-SIGHT

Option 1: a point-to-point wireless link from the main house

If your farmhouse, barn or main house already has broadband and you can see the hut from it (or from a shed or pole somewhere in between), this is almost always the best option. We fix a small weatherproof antenna at each end and beam the connection across — no trenches, no digging and no second monthly bill.

This is exactly the kind of link we’ve been trusted with before: in 2015 we installed a point-to-point wireless link across the River Avon to carry data for the Environment Agency’s fish counter at Knapp Mill, and we still support it today. A shepherd’s hut at the bottom of a field is comfortably within reach.

  • Best value once installed — it rides on broadband you already pay for.
  • Fast and low-latency, good for video calls, streaming and card machines.
  • Needs a reasonably clear line-of-sight, which we check on the survey.

// MOBILE SIGNAL

Option 2: a 4G or 5G router with a proper external antenna

Where there’s no clear line-of-sight back to a building — trees, a hill or simply too great a distance in the way — but your phone still gets a bar or two, a 4G or 5G router is often the simplest answer. The trick is not to rely on the little aerial inside the router: we fit a proper external antenna, usually on the roof or a short pole, aimed at the best mast.

  • Uses a data SIM — we’ll help you pick a sensible tariff for the usage.
  • An external, directional antenna can turn one weak bar into a steady, usable connection.
  • Ideal for holiday lets where guests just need to stream and browse.

On the survey we test the actual signal at the hut, not the signal out on the road, so you get an honest read before anything is ordered.

// TRULY REMOTE

Option 3: Starlink for sites with no signal and no line-of-sight

For huts that are genuinely off the map — deep in a valley, out on the heath, no mobile signal and nothing to see from the main house — Starlink is the option that almost always works. It talks to satellites overhead, so all it needs is a fairly open view of the sky and some power.

  • Works where both 4G and line-of-sight fail.
  • Needs a spot with an open view of the sky and a modest amount of power to run.
  • We can supply, install, align and secure the dish, then set up a tidy Wi-Fi network inside the hut.

It’s the priciest of the three to run month to month, so we’ll only steer you here if the cheaper options genuinely won’t reach.

// NO MAINS?

Running it off-grid, plus guest Wi-Fi for holiday lets

Plenty of shepherd’s huts and glamping pods have no mains electricity at all — and that’s no barrier. A router, a 4G unit or even a Starlink dish draws only a modest amount of power, which a small solar panel and a lithium battery can supply quietly, day and night.

We design, supply, install and remotely monitor off-grid Victron solar power and leisure & marine lithium batteries, so your internet, lights and phone-charging can all run from the same tidy setup — with no generator droning away.

Running a holiday let? We set up a separate guest Wi-Fi network so visitors get their own password while your own devices, cameras or booking system stay private and secure. Ask us about wider coverage on our rural & farm Wi-Fi service too.

// GOOD QUESTIONS

Frequently asked

How do I get Wi-Fi to a shepherd’s hut with no cable running to it?

There are three routes, depending on your site: a point-to-point wireless link if you can see a building that already has broadband, a 4G or 5G router with an external antenna if there’s mobile signal, or Starlink if the hut is truly remote. We survey the site first and recommend the honest best fit.

Can I get internet to a glamping pod with no mobile signal at all?

Yes. Where there’s no signal and no line-of-sight back to a building, Starlink satellite broadband will almost always work, as long as the pod has a fairly open view of the sky and a little power to run the dish.

How far can a point-to-point wireless link reach?

With a clear line-of-sight it can carry a connection a very long way — we run one that crosses the River Avon for the Environment Agency’s fish counter. A hut across a field or paddock is comfortably within range; we confirm the exact path on the survey.

Do you install shepherd’s hut Wi-Fi across Dorset and the New Forest?

Yes. We’re based at Kinson in Bournemouth and cover Bournemouth, Poole, Christchurch and the wider Dorset area, plus the edge of the New Forest — Lymington, Ringwood and New Milton — for on-site work like this.

Can the Wi-Fi run if the hut has no mains electricity?

Yes. A router, 4G unit or Starlink dish uses only a modest amount of power, so we can run it from a small Victron solar panel and lithium battery. That keeps your internet going day and night with no generator.

Can holiday-let guests have their own separate Wi-Fi?

Absolutely. We set up a separate guest network with its own password, so visitors can get online while your own devices, cameras and booking system stay private. It’s the safe way to share Wi-Fi in a let.
