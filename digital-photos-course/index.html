Your photos, safe and sorted

A free, friendly course from the family-run firm that has been untangling technology in Bournemouth since 1995. Six short lessons show you where your photos really live, how to back them up so a lost phone never means lost memories, and how to find, share, print and tidy them — all at your own pace, with a printable certificate at the end. No jargon, nothing to install, nothing to pay.

Welcome

// WHAT’S IN THIS COURSE

6 short lessons, at your own pace

Every lesson ends with one gentle “what would you do?” question — no scores, no time limits, no pass marks. Here’s what you’ll cover:

  1. Where your photos actually live. Here is the single most important idea in this course, and it is worth a cup of tea and a moment’s thought: a photo on your phone normally lives in the phone itself , on a little memory chip inside the handset.
  2. Backing up without tears. On Android phones, the usual tool for the job is the Google Photos app — its icon looks like a small, colourful pinwheel.
  3. Finding photos again. Once your photos are safe, the next pleasure is finding them again.
  4. Sharing the joy. Photos double in value when they are shared.
  5. Printing them. A photo on a screen is lovely; a photo on the mantelpiece is part of the furniture.
  6. A tidy-up that frees your phone. Sooner or later, every phone complains that its storage is nearly full — and photos are usually the reason.

// KEEP THIS BY THE COMPUTER

Your one-page checklist

The whole course, boiled down to the reminders that matter. Print it and keep it near the computer.

  1. A photo on your phone is usually the only copy in the world — until a backup is switched on.
  2. Check your backup in Google Photos: tap your profile picture or initial and look for the word “backup” — you want “on” or “complete”.
  3. Backup is free within your Google account’s 15 GB allowance (shared with Gmail and Google Drive), at the time of writing.
  4. The cloud simply means a second copy kept on Google’s secure computers — your photos still appear on the phone as normal.
  5. Find any photo with the search box: try a place, a month, a year, or a word like “dog” or “beach”.
  6. Gather the special ones into albums — the digital version of the album on the shelf.
  7. Share one or two photos by WhatsApp; share a whole occasion as an album, so everyone sees the full set in one place.
  8. Only share album links with people you know — anyone holding a link may be able to view it.
  9. Supermarkets and high-street chemists print straight from your phone or a USB stick — and photo books make wonderful gifts.
  10. The golden rule: never delete photos until backup is confirmed — then tidy freely, or use the free-up-space feature.

// GOOD QUESTIONS

Frequently asked

Is this course really free?

Completely free, with no catch and no card details asked for. We are a family-run IT firm that has looked after Bournemouth homes and businesses since 1995, and this course is our way of helping local people keep the photos that matter most to them.

Do I need to pay for photo storage?

Usually not. Every Google account comes with 15 GB of free storage (shared with Gmail and Google Drive) at the time of writing, which holds thousands of ordinary phone photos. If you are a very keen photographer and ever approach the limit, paid options exist — but most people never need them, and we are happy to advise if you do.

I have an Apple iPhone — is this course still for me?

The big ideas apply to every phone: back up first, use albums for the special ones, share kindly, print the keepers, and never delete until the backup is confirmed. The step-by-step details here are written for Android phones, but Apple has its own equivalents of everything mentioned — Apple’s own guides and a confident family member are the best companions for the iPhone-specific steps, and every habit this course teaches will serve you just as well there.

Is the cloud actually safe? What if my photos disappear?

A backed-up photo lives on professionally run, well-guarded computers — and it still lives on your phone too, so you have two copies instead of one. No arrangement in life is beyond all doubt, but two copies are enormously safer than the single copy most people rely on. For the handful of truly irreplaceable pictures, a printed copy in a frame or album is a lovely third layer of protection.

What happens to my photos when I get a new phone?

This is where backup pays for itself. Sign in to the new phone with the same Google account, open Google Photos, and your backed-up pictures appear — the whole collection, ready and waiting. A broken or lost phone works the same way: the photos were never really in the handset once backup was on; they were safe in your account all along.

Can someone help me set all this up in person?

Gladly. Ring us on 01202 775566 and we will happily check your backup over the phone or arrange a visit — and we run friendly, unhurried in-person lessons at the Kinson Community Centre in Bournemouth, where checking a photo backup is one of the most popular requests. Bring your phone and your questions.

Rather learn with a friendly human?

We run patient, plain-English computer lessons at the Kinson Community Centre — and we’re on the phone Mon–Fri, 9–5. No silly questions, ever.
