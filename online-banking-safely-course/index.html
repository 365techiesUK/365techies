Online banking, without the worry

Many people avoid online banking because of scare stories — this free course gives you the honest picture, and the few golden rules that genuinely keep your money safe. Work through seven short lessons at your own pace, then print your certificate with your name on it. No jargon, no rush, no catch.

Welcome

// WHAT’S IN THIS COURSE

7 short lessons, at your own pace

Every lesson ends with one gentle “what would you do?” question — no scores, no time limits, no pass marks. Here’s what you’ll cover:

  1. Why everyone keeps mentioning online banking. If your local branch has closed, you are not alone — a great many bank branches have shut across the UK in recent years, and banks now do most of their day-to-day work through the phone in your pocket or the computer on your desk.
  2. The app or the website?. There are two front doors to online banking: the bank’s app (a button on your phone or tablet) and the bank’s website (typed into a computer).
  3. Setting up, gently. Here is the most reassuring thing we tell people at our lessons in Kinson: you cannot break your bank account by looking at it.
  4. The golden rules your bank lives by. These few rules are worth more than everything else in this course put together.
  5. Paying someone: the name check. When you set up a payment to someone new, most UK banks now run a helpful check called Confirmation of Payee .
  6. The bank that isn’t your bank. Bank-impersonation scams almost always arrive one of two ways: an urgent phone call (“your account has been compromised”) or an alarming text (“a payment has been attempted — click here to verify”).
  7. The five-minute habit that keeps you safe. Here is online banking’s best-kept secret: it can make you safer than paper statements ever did, because you can look at your account any day you like — not once a month when the envelope arrives.

// KEEP THIS BY THE COMPUTER

Your one-page checklist

The whole course, boiled down to the reminders that matter. Print it and keep it near the computer.

  1. Get your banking app only from the Google Play Store on Android
  2. Never log in through a link sent by text or email
  3. Your bank will NEVER ask you to move money to a safe account
  4. One-time codes are for typing into screens, never for reading aloud
  5. Never give anyone your full PIN or full password
  6. Suspicious call from the bank? Hang up, wait, then dial 159
  7. Forward scam texts free to 7726, then delete them
  8. Paying someone new? Check the name matches and triple-check the details
  9. Glance through your payments each week and ring the bank about anything odd
  10. Stuck or worried? Call 365 Techies on 01202 775566

// GOOD QUESTIONS

Frequently asked

Is online banking really safe for someone my age?

Yes — genuinely. UK banks are strictly regulated, an official compensation scheme stands behind the money in your account (up to a set limit), and payments you never authorised would normally have to be refunded. The real risk isn’t the technology; it’s scammers on the phone pretending to be your bank — and spotting them is exactly what this course teaches.

Do I need a computer, or will my phone do?

A smartphone or tablet is all you need — for most people, the bank’s own app is the easiest and safest way in. Everything in this course works on Android phones and Windows computers; Apple devices have their own equivalents.

What happens if I press the wrong button?

Nothing dramatic, we promise. Simply looking around your account can’t move any money — a payment always needs several deliberate steps and a final confirmation. You can explore to your heart’s content.

Is this course really free? What’s the catch?

Completely free — no card details, no trial, no catch. We’re a family IT firm in Bournemouth, going since 1995, and we’d simply rather local people were safe and confident. If you ever want hands-on help, we hope you’ll think of us.

Do I get a certificate, and is it a qualification?

When you finish, you can type in your first name and print a 365 Techies certificate of completion. It’s a lovely thing to keep on the fridge, though not a formal qualification — the real prize is the confidence.

What if I get stuck partway through?

Help is one phone call away — ring us on 01202 775566 and a friendly human will talk it through with you. And if you’d rather learn face to face, we run in-person lessons at the Kinson Community Centre in Bournemouth.

Rather learn with a friendly human?

We run patient, plain-English computer lessons at the Kinson Community Centre — and we’re on the phone Mon–Fri, 9–5. No silly questions, ever.
