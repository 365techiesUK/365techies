Credit where it's due

Still running? That’s the whole point

The Latitude E5550 is a 15.6-inch business laptop launched in late 2014, with a second wave in early 2015. If yours still boots every morning, it’s roughly eleven years old and still working — which is remarkable, and not an accident.

Latitudes were built for corporate fleets: sturdier hinges, better keyboards and longer-lasting components than the consumer laptops sold alongside them. That durability is precisely why we’ve sold business-grade Dell since 2001, and why every replacement we supply is a refurbished business-grade Latitude rather than a new budget machine. Your E5550 is the proof that the approach works.

So this isn’t a page telling you your laptop was rubbish. It wasn’t. It’s a page about what happens when a genuinely good machine simply runs out of road.

The honest bit

Three real reasons it’s time to move on

We’re not in the business of scaring people into buying things. But there are three facts about an eleven-year-old laptop that we’d be doing you a disservice to gloss over:

  • The hard drive is living on borrowed time. Most E5550s shipped with a mechanical hard drive — a spinning disk with moving parts, now over a decade old. When these fail, they often fail suddenly, taking photos and documents with them. If your files aren’t backed up, this is the risk that matters most.
  • The memory struggles with the modern web. Many E5550s carry 4GB or 8GB of older DDR3 memory. A modern browser with a handful of tabs can swallow that on its own, which is why everything feels slower than it used to — the machine hasn’t got worse, the internet has got heavier.
  • It cannot run Windows 11 — and this is the big one. The E5550 uses 4th- or 5th-generation Intel processors, depending on when it was built. Windows 11 officially requires 8th-generation Intel or newer, plus TPM 2.0, so this machine doesn’t qualify. And Windows 10 support ended on 14 October 2025, meaning no more free security updates. Every month an unsupported Windows 10 machine stays online, the security risk grows.

Offline or very light use? Different story — more on that below. But for everyday online use, the Windows situation alone settles it.

The question everyone asks

Can’t I just force Windows 11 onto it?

Honest answer: yes, the bypass tricks exist. There are registry edits and installer workarounds that skip the TPM and processor checks, and plenty of guides online will walk you through them.

We recommend against it, and here’s why — not because we want to sell you a laptop, but because we’d have to say the same thing to our own family:

  • Updates aren’t guaranteed. Microsoft states that unsupported installs may not receive updates — including security updates. You could go through the whole exercise and end up no safer than you were on Windows 10.
  • The hardware isn’t up to it anyway. Even if Windows 11 installs, you’re running a heavier operating system on a decade-old drive and modest memory. It will feel worse, not better.
  • You inherit the risk. If a future update refuses to install or breaks the machine, there’s no supported path to fix it.

A bypass spends effort making an old machine limp along. The same money and less hassle gets you a properly supported machine that’s dramatically faster. We’ve written more on this at “This PC can’t run Windows 11” on a Dell.

Triage, not sales patter

What we’d honestly do with your E5550

Before anyone mentions a replacement, we do an honest triage — our upgrade services are available on request, and the first step is always a straight conversation about how you actually use the machine.

  • Offline or very light use? If your E5550 never goes online — a writing machine, a workshop laptop, something running one old program — then a modest SSD upgrade can genuinely make sense, and we’ll say so. It won’t make it a modern computer, but it removes the failing-drive risk and gives it a spring in its step for what it does.
  • Everyday online use? Then no upgrade fixes the real problem: the processor can never meet Windows 11’s requirements, and Windows 10’s security updates have stopped. Spending on RAM and an SSD here is money towards a dead end, and we’d rather tell you that than take it.

Frequently asked

Can the Dell Latitude E5550 run Windows 11?

Not officially, no. The E5550 uses 4th- or 5th-generation Intel processors, and Windows 11 requires 8th-generation Intel or newer plus TPM 2.0. Bypass tricks exist, but Microsoft doesn’t guarantee updates on unsupported installs, so we honestly advise against them.

Is the Latitude E5550 still good in 2026?

As a testament to Dell’s business build quality, absolutely — it’s roughly eleven years old. For everyday online use, honestly, no: Windows 10 security updates ended on 14 October 2025, the original hard drive is a decade-old mechanical part, and the memory struggles with a modern browser. For offline or very light use, it can still earn its keep.

Windows 10 has ended — is it dangerous to keep using my E5550 online?

It’s a growing risk rather than an overnight cliff-edge. Since 14 October 2025 there have been no free security updates, so newly discovered flaws stay unpatched. For a machine used for email, banking or shopping, that risk compounds every month. We’ve set out the options on our Windows 10 ESU or upgrade page.

What should I replace a Latitude E5550 with?

The natural successors are the 15.6-inch Latitude 3520 (guide £545) or Latitude 5520 (guide £535) — same family, several generations newer. Both come with 16GB of memory, a new 1TB Samsung 990 PRO SSD, Windows 11 set up, your data transferred, and a 12-month guarantee. You can pick one and get a quote online.

Is it worth putting an SSD in a Latitude E5550?

Sometimes — and we’ll tell you straight. If the machine lives offline or does one light job, an SSD removes the failing-drive risk and speeds it up nicely. If it’s your everyday online computer, an SSD can’t fix the real problem: no Windows 11 support and no more Windows 10 security updates.

Will you move my files from the old laptop to the new one?

Yes — data transfer is included with every refurbished Dell we supply. Documents, photos, favourites and email are moved across and checked. Your old E5550’s drive is then securely wiped and the machine responsibly recycled.
