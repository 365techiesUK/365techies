Who we are

Independent XPS repair — without couriering it to a London mail-in shop

We’ve been a family-run computer business since 1995, we hold a 4.9 rating on Google, and we work Monday to Friday, 9am–5pm. There’s no walk-in shop — we come to you instead. Honest advice

A premium laptop deserves a straight answer, not a sales pitch

Here’s our position, plainly: we don’t stock XPS machines, so we have nothing to gain by talking you out of a repair — and nothing to gain by talking you into one either. Our refurbished stock is business-grade Latitude and OptiPlex; if you want a new XPS we can order one to spec, but we make our living from honest repair work and support plans, not from replacing laptops that didn’t need replacing.

XPS owners usually want to repair. These are lovely machines — the carbon-fibre decks, the near-borderless screens — and a well-looked-after XPS 13 or 15 has years left in it after a new battery or a proper thermal service.

But we’ll also be honest about the other side: XPS parts are premium parts. A 4K or OLED touch panel for an XPS costs a lot more than a standard laptop screen, and on an older machine that can push a quote past what the laptop is sensibly worth. When that happens we say so before you spend anything — diagnosis first, written quote, your decision. We quote repair work as a range after we’ve actually looked at the machine; we don’t do invented fixed fees.

Common faults

The XPS faults we’re asked about most

These are the patterns that come up again and again with XPS machines — every one of them documented at length on Dell’s own support pages and community forums, not invented by us.

The touchpad is lifting, or the case won’t sit flat — swollen battery

The best-known age-related XPS failure, especially on XPS 13 models from the 9350/9360/9370 era. As the lithium battery ages it can swell, deforming the chassis from the inside — a bulging underside, a warped keyboard deck, a touchpad that’s pushed up and clicks wrongly. Dell’s own community forums carry long threads on exactly this. It isn’t a recall situation; it’s a known age-related failure — but it is a safety issue. If your XPS shows these signs, stop charging it and get the battery replaced promptly. This is one of our most common XPS jobs and it usually saves the machine completely.

A high-pitched electrical whine — coil whine

XPS 13 and XPS 15 owners have reported coil whine — a faint, high-pitched noise from the electronics under load — across many generations, in long-running Dell Community threads spanning many generations. Honesty required here: coil whine is usually behaviour at board level, and Dell forum users have reported it surviving multiple motherboard swaps. We can often reduce it meaningfully with power and turbo settings, and we’ll check nothing else is masquerading as it (a tired fan often is) — but we won’t promise to eliminate it, because nobody honestly can.

An XPS 15 that runs hot and slows down under load

The XPS 15’s slim chassis packs a lot of power behind a compact cooling system, and reviewers such as NotebookCheck documented thermal throttling on generations from the 9550 through to the 9530 — the machine gets hot, then quietly drops performance to cope. At two or three years old, dust-clogged fans and dried factory thermal paste make it worse. A proper thermal service — strip-down, dust-out, fresh paste, and thermal-pad attention where appropriate — is a genuine, well-evidenced fix that restores lost performance. We’re honest about limits: a thin premium laptop will always run warmer than a thick workhorse.

Wi-Fi that keeps dropping — the Killer wireless card

Many XPS generations shipped with Killer-brand Wi-Fi cards, and Dell publishes its own troubleshooting guide for them because dropped connections are so widely reported. Sometimes it’s cured in software — taming the Killer prioritisation utilities and updating drivers. Where it isn’t, the community-proven fix is swapping the card for an Intel wireless module — a small, worthwhile job we’re happy to do, and far cheaper than living with unusable Wi-Fi.

XPS 13 Plus: the touch function row or touchpad stops responding

The XPS 13 Plus (9320) replaced physical function keys with a capacitive touch strip and hid the touchpad under a seamless glass palm rest. Dell’s own UK support pages document the touch function row becoming unresponsive (often when warm, or after sleep), with a BIOS update as the first fix; owners also report the invisible touchpad ghosting or going dead. We update firmware, isolate whether it’s software or hardware, and quote honestly from there. Telling detail: when Dell relaunched the XPS line for 2026, it brought back real function keys and a visible touchpad.

Upgrades & servicing

What can — and honestly can’t — be upgraded on an XPS

The single most important thing a repair firm can tell an XPS owner: on recent XPS 13 models, the RAM is soldered to the motherboard and cannot be upgraded — ever. Dell’s own service manuals confirm it. The XPS 15 and 17 are different: they use standard SODIMM slots, so a memory upgrade there is straightforward. We always check the service manual for your exact model before quoting, because generalising about XPS upgrades is how people waste money.

  • SSD upgrades — the storage drive is user-replaceable on most XPS models (the 13 Plus uses the shorter M.2 2230 size, which catches people out). A larger, faster SSD upgrade with your data transferred across is often the best value-per-pound improvement an XPS can get.
  • Battery replacement — whether it’s swollen or simply worn out and lasting an hour, a new battery is routine for us and transforms an older XPS.
  • Thermal service — strip-down, dust removal and fresh thermal paste, particularly worthwhile on hard-working XPS 15s.
  • Screen replacement — cracked and damaged XPS screens are repairable, with one honest caveat: high-end 4K, OLED and touch panels are expensive parts, and on older machines we’ll tell you plainly if the numbers don’t stack up before you commit.
  • Wi-Fi card replacement — swapping a troublesome Killer card for an Intel module, as above.

Everything is diagnosis-first: we look, we quote in writing, you decide.

XPS in 2026

Is Dell XPS discontinued? The strange 2025–2026 story

If you’ve searched “is Dell XPS discontinued” and found contradictory answers, that’s because both answers were true at different points — within about twelve months of each other.

At CES in January 2025, Dell announced it was retiring the XPS name after some thirty years, folding its premium laptops into a renamed line-up — the 2025-generation machines shipped badged “Dell 13/14/16 Premium” rather than XPS. Then at CES 2026, Dell publicly changed course, and XPS returned as a current name: the new XPS 14 and XPS 16 went on sale in January 2026 — notably with physical function keys and a conventional touchpad restored, reversing the XPS 13 Plus’s most divisive design choices.

So as things stand in 2026, based on Dell’s announcements and the coverage around them:

  • XPS is a live brand again for new machines;
  • “Dell Premium” badges appear only on the 2025 interim generation — if you own a Dell 14 Premium or 16 Premium, it’s the same premium consumer line and we service it exactly the same way;
  • and the vast installed base — XPS 13, 15, 17, 13 Plus and the XPS 8000-series desktops — never stopped being XPS at all.

Naming is Dell’s business and it has moved more than once recently, so we’d always check Dell’s own site for the current line-up before ordering new. From a repair point of view, none of it matters: whatever the lid says, it’s the same machine underneath, and we look after them all.

If the honest answer is “replace” — here are your real options

Sometimes the diagnosis-first process ends with us saying a repair isn’t good value. When that happens, here’s the truthful picture of what we can and can’t offer:

We don’t stock XPS machines — new or refurbished. Our refurbished stock is business-grade Dell: Latitude laptops from £510 and OptiPlex desktops from £545 (guide prices, each fitted with a new 1TB Samsung 990 PRO SSD, set up on Windows 11 with your data transferred, 12-month return-to-base warranty — extended to 5 years on a support plan). An honest suitability note: a refurbished Latitude is a sturdy, dependable workhorse, but it won’t match an XPS for screen quality or slimness — it suits “I need a solid laptop for work and life”, not “I want another premium ultrabook”.

If only an XPS will do, we can order a new one to spec — as an independent Dell reseller since 2001 we can supply any current Dell line, including the relaunched XPS models. (New premium laptops cost more than they used to; we’ve written honestly about why computer prices have gone up.)

The Windows deadline matters here too. Windows 10 support ended on 14 October 2025, and the Extended Security Updates bridge runs out on 12 October 2027. If an older XPS can’t move to Windows 11, that’s a real factor in the keep-or-replace sum — our guide to Windows 10 ESU or upgrading your Dell walks through the options without scaremongering. And whichever way you go, we handle the old machine responsibly with secure IT disposal — wiped properly, recycled properly.

Frequently asked

Is my Dell XPS worth repairing?

Usually, yes — more often than most laptops, in fact. XPS machines are well-built premium laptops, and a new battery, an SSD upgrade or a proper thermal service often buys years of extra life for far less than a comparable new machine. The honest exception is major damage on older models: premium parts like 4K or OLED screens are expensive, and we’ll tell you plainly when a quote doesn’t make sense. Diagnosis comes first, so you decide with real numbers in front of you.

My XPS 13’s touchpad is lifting and the case is bulging — is that serious?

Yes — those are the classic signs of a swollen lithium battery, a well-documented age-related failure on XPS 13 models (the 9350, 9360 and 9370 era especially). A swollen battery is a safety issue: stop charging the laptop and get the battery replaced promptly. It’s a routine repair for us, and in most cases the machine is completely fine once the new battery is in.

Can you fix coil whine on a Dell XPS?

We’ll be straight with you: often we can reduce it, but nobody can honestly promise to eliminate it. Coil whine on the XPS 13 and 15 is widely reported on Dell’s own forums across many generations, and it’s usually behaviour at board level — owners have reported it surviving motherboard replacements. We can tune power and turbo settings to quieten it and rule out other causes like a failing fan, and we’ll tell you honestly what improvement to expect before you spend anything.

Can the RAM in a Dell XPS 13 be upgraded?

No — on recent XPS 13 models the memory is soldered to the motherboard, so it can never be upgraded. Dell’s service manuals confirm this. The XPS 15 and 17 are different: they have standard memory slots and can usually be upgraded. The SSD is user-replaceable on most XPS models, so storage upgrades are almost always possible. We check the service manual for your exact model before quoting anything.

Is the Dell XPS discontinued?

It was — briefly. Dell announced the retirement of the XPS name in January 2025, and the 2025-generation machines shipped as “Dell Premium” instead. Then at CES 2026 Dell reversed course, and new XPS 14 and XPS 16 laptops went on sale in January 2026. So XPS is a current brand again. From a repair point of view it never mattered: we service every generation of XPS, and the 2025 “Dell Premium” machines too — same line, same service.

I have a Dell 14 Premium — is that an XPS?

Effectively, yes. The Dell 13/14/16 Premium machines are the 2025 interim generation of Dell’s premium consumer line — the models built during the year the XPS name was retired, before Dell brought it back for 2026. They’re the same category of machine and we repair, upgrade and support them exactly as we do any XPS.

Do you have a shop I can bring my XPS into?

No — there’s no walk-in shop, and that’s deliberate: we come to you instead. We collect free of charge across Bournemouth, Poole, Christchurch and the wider Dorset area, fix a great deal by secure remote support, and visit businesses on-site. If you’d like to meet in person we can arrange an appointment at Kinson Community Centre. For a £1,500 laptop, free doorstep collection beats posting it to a mail-in shop.

Do you sell refurbished XPS laptops?

No — and we’d rather say so plainly than fudge it. Our refurbished stock is business-grade Dell Latitude laptops (from £510) and OptiPlex desktops (from £545), each with a new 1TB Samsung 990 PRO SSD, Windows 11 setup and data transfer included. A Latitude is a sturdy workhorse but it isn’t a premium ultrabook, and we’ll say that too. If only an XPS will do, we can order a new one to your spec as an independent Dell reseller.

My XPS is still on Windows 10 — what should I do?

Windows 10 support ended on 14 October 2025, and the Extended Security Updates bridge runs only until 12 October 2027 — so this needs a decision within the year. Many XPS machines can upgrade to Windows 11; some older ones can’t. We’ll check your exact model and talk you through the honest options: upgrade, bridge with ESU, or factor it into a replacement decision. Our Windows 10 ESU guide covers it in plain English.

How much does a Dell XPS repair cost?

We don’t quote invented fixed fees, because honest pricing on premium machines depends on the exact model and part. The process is: free local collection, diagnosis first, then a written quote as a clear range before any work happens. Batteries, SSD upgrades and thermal services are usually very good value; high-end screens cost more, and if a repair isn’t worth it on an older machine, we’ll tell you so before you spend a penny.
