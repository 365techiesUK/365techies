💡 Golden rules (read before you build)

  • Fuse every positive as close to the battery as practical — the fuse is there to protect the cable, not the gadget.
  • Fat and short beats long and thin. Keep inverter cables as short as possible, and if you parallel batteries, keep the pair’s leads equal length.
  • Crimp, don’t solder. Soldered joints crack with vehicle vibration; a properly crimped lug with adhesive heatshrink lasts decades.
  • Put a battery isolator switch on the main positive — one twist to make the whole system safe to work on.
  • Everything negative goes through the shunt. One cable from the battery negative to the shunt, and every load’s negative on the far side — or your state-of-charge reading is fiction.
  • Torque terminals to the manual’s spec and re-check them after your first weekend away — connections bed in.
  • Ventilate and protect from temperature. Lithium won’t charge below 0°C (the SuperPack protects itself) — inside the living space beats an unheated garage box.
  • Know where DIY ends. 12V is friendly; the 230V side (hook-up, sockets, RCDs) is qualified-electrician territory, and gas is Gas Safe territory. No exceptions.

// GOOD QUESTIONS

Frequently asked

How is the kit sized?

From your daily energy use: watts × hours plus 15% real-world losses, a 1.5-day battery buffer at lithium’s ~90% usable capacity, solar sized for UK spring/autumn sun (~2.5 peak hours), and the matching Victron MPPT from the official controller limits. It’s an honest starter spec — a real design also covers cable sizing, fusing and alternator limits.

Are the shop links affiliate links?

No — the buttons simply open live searches on Amazon UK and Bimble Solar for each part, and we don’t earn a penny from them. If that ever changes we’ll say so clearly right next to the links. We recommend this kit because we install it and run it ourselves.

How accurate are the price guides?

They’re typical UK retail ranges (as of mid-2026) meant for planning a budget, not quoting a job — Victron prices move with sales and stock, so the live links always show today’s real price. Expect the truth to land inside the range most of the time, and add the 10–15% cabling allowance on top.

Why Victron?

It’s the kit we trust with our own van: everything talks to everything (one app for the lot), the build quality is superb, warranties are long, and the remote-monitoring platform (VRM) is genuinely brilliant — you can watch our own system streaming live on this website.

Can I fit it myself?

Plenty of people do — 12V systems are DIY-friendly if you’re careful, and the SuperPack batteries with built-in protection make it safer. The parts that deserve respect are fusing, cable sizing and anything near the alternator or mains. We’re happy to fit kit you’ve bought, check over a DIY install, or do the whole job.

Will you price-match or supply the kit?

We’ll quote for supply-and-fit with warranty and remote monitoring set up — and we’ll tell you honestly if buying parts yourself and having us fit them works out better for you. No games; just ask.

What about winter?

UK winter gives roughly one hour of usable sun a day — an eighth of summer — so winter systems lean on engine charging (the Orion B2B) or hook-up. The builder already assumes that; don’t let anyone sell you a roof full of panels as a winter solution.

Where do the cable and fuse sizes come from?

From Victron’s own manuals and wiring guidance, stated for short runs (≤3m one way) at 12V. Two rules matter above all: the fuse protects the cable (never fit a bigger fuse than the cable’s rating), and longer runs need thicker cable to beat voltage drop. Our design service calculates exact runs for your layout.

What tools do I really need?

The one that matters most is a proper hydraulic lug crimper — a bad crimp on a 70mm² inverter cable is a fire risk, a good one lasts decades. The builder lists the essential kit and the nice-to-haves, with links. Or borrow ours: we fit customer-supplied kit too.

How to size Victron campervan cables and fuses

Answer four questions and this free builder returns a complete Victron campervan system with the exact cable, lug and fuse size for every circuit — sized for short runs (up to 3m one way) at 12V — following the golden rule that the fuse protects the cable, so you never fit a fuse rated higher than its cable.

Victron 12V cable & fuse guide — short runs (≤3m one way). Golden rule: the fuse protects the cable, never fit a fuse rated above its cable.

Step by step

  1. Set your daily energy — Pick a preset (weekender ~350Wh, regular ~800Wh, full-timer ~1500Wh) or fine-tune the Wh/day slider — this drives the battery, solar and cable sizing.
  2. Choose your biggest mains load — Say whether you run all 12V, a laptop/TV inverter, a microwave, or a kettle/induction — this sets the main battery cable and MEGA/Class-T fuse size.
  3. Add engine and monitoring — Choose whether you charge from the engine (adds an Orion B2B circuit) and your monitoring level, so every relevant circuit appears.
  4. Read the per-circuit table — Scroll to ‘Cables, lugs & fuses — per circuit’ for the cable mm², fuse and lug for the main battery, negative/shunt, solar, PV, engine and 12V distribution runs.
  5. Apply the golden rules — Fuse every positive close to the battery, keep inverter cable fat and short, crimp don’t solder, and upsize cable for runs longer than 3m to beat voltage drop.

Good to know

What size cable and fuse do I need for a campervan inverter?

It depends on the inverter: the builder specs 25mm² cable with a 100A MEGA fuse for an ~800W Phoenix, 70mm² with a 300A MEGA fuse for a 2000W MultiPlus, and twin 70mm² per pole with a 400A Class-T fuse for a 3000W kettle-capable unit. In every case the fuse protects the cable, so it is never rated above the cable’s capacity.

What fuse goes on a Victron Orion B2B engine-charging circuit?

For the Orion XS 12/12-50 the builder specifies 16mm² tinned cable for runs up to 3m (25mm² for longer) fused with a 60A MIDI fuse at BOTH ends — at the starter battery and the leisure battery — because that cable runs the length of the vehicle and must be protected at each end.

Do longer cable runs need a bigger cable in a 12V system?

Yes. The sizes shown are for short runs of up to 3m one way at 12V; longer runs need thicker cable to beat voltage drop, while the fuse stays matched to the cable it protects. Use tinned marine cable and adhesive-lined heatshrink on every lug; for exact runs on your layout our design service calculates them properly.
