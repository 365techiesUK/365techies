Bournemouth weather: the next 10 days

The sea is measured, not forecast →

    A forecast is a prediction, so it changes. The first two to three days come in hourly steps and are the ones to plan around; after that the model works in six-hour steps and the detail is looser, so check again nearer the day. Rain is the forecast amount in millimetres — this forecast gives no percentage chance for our coast, so we don’t invent one. Wind is the forecast average speed, not gusts.

    Full seafront weather: tide times, wind, rain radar and satellite →

    Forecast data from MET Norway (the Norwegian Meteorological Institute) for Bournemouth Pier, licensed under CC BY 4.0. Changes made: we group their hourly and six-hourly steps into days, round the numbers, convert wind to mph and draw our own symbols. It updates about every hour.

    Weather, tides & radar

    The seafront forecast hour by hour and ten days out, tide times checked against the gauge on the pier, rain radar and satellite loops every 15 minutes, and the wind measured at the airport — every number labelled with where it came from.
    The live map

    Buses, flights, road closures, river levels, bike bays and the latest satellite pass over Bournemouth, Christchurch and Poole — every layer from a named public feed, on a free flat map, with the 3D city loaded only when you ask for it.
    The sea right now

    Sea temperature and waves measured by the bay’s buoy, tide from the gauge on the pier, water quality for all seven beaches — live, timestamped, never modelled.
    Friday fireworks

    The 2026 season, every date and what happened — three Fridays lost to the wildfire emergency and high winds, one finale added — and the best places to stand when the displays return.
    Sunrise & sunset

    Today’s times computed for the seafront, and every good spot to watch from — photographed by us, not stock.
    Beach parking, honestly

    What it really costs, where it is far cheaper, and the rules that catch people out — every price from BCP’s own pages, dated.
    Mobile signal, measured

    Real 4G/5G speeds our own van logs as it drives around Bournemouth and Poole — the places that tested fastest for working on the move, on a map you can check before you rely on it. One van, one network, measured not modelled.
    Test your own phone here

    Ten seconds, where you’re standing: your phone’s real mobile-data speed, how it compares with your part of town, and a live map everyone’s readings build together. No sign-up, nothing that identifies you — and no network league tables.

    What this is

    For years our Bournemouth365 Facebook page (born Bournemouth Live) has filmed this coastline daily — the calm mornings, the storms, the fireworks, the light. These pages bring that to the open web and add the thing social media can’t: live measured data with its provenance shown. The sea page reads real instruments — the bay’s wave buoy, the Environment Agency’s tide gauge mounted on Bournemouth Pier — and every reading carries the time it was taken. When a feed is down, the page says so rather than guessing.

    The weather panel at the top is the one thing here that is a forecast, and it is labelled as one on every screen: MET Norway’s model for Bournemouth Pier, with the time it was issued. Still to come: a page on Westover Road in 1985 — the cinemas, the ice rink and the cruising loop — built from sourced local history. Built in Bournemouth by 365 Techies, the family firm that has looked after the town’s computers since 1995.

    No ads. No paywall. No consent wall. Every reading carries its instrument and its measurement time.
