What will Windows say when I open it?

Usually nothing: it’s digitally signed by 365 Techies Ltd. But a brand-new program can get a warning or two while Windows gets to know it. When downloading, Edge may say the file “isn’t commonly downloaded”: press the three dots next to it, choose Keep, then Show more and Keep anyway. When you open it, you may see a blue “Windows protected your PC” box: choose More info, check that the Publisher line says 365 Techies Ltd, then choose Run anyway. If the publisher says anything else, don’t run it, and ring us.

Will I lose my old emails?

Usually not. If your old program used the normal setting (IMAP), your emails are kept with your email provider, so they appear in Techies One, folders and all, as soon as you add your address. Then Bring in old emails can copy them from the old program’s files (Windows Live Mail, Windows Mail, Thunderbird or an Outlook .pst file; Outlook Express files are still experimental) into a mailbox on this PC, without changing the originals. Keep the old program or PC until they’re in, and ask us if you’re not sure.

// MAIL

Mail: the simple replacement for Windows Mail and Outlook Express

Email programs have looked much the same for twenty years. Techies One Mail was built around the problems our customers actually ring us about: scams, settings that won’t take, an inbox they can’t keep on top of, and too many buttons. This film shows it fixing them.

Several email addresses to keep an eye on

All your inboxes in one list

Got a BT address, a Gmail and a work one? Add them all, and All inboxes shows the email from every one of them in one list, newest first, each marked with the address it came to. Reply, and it goes from the address the email was sent to, so you never answer from the wrong one. The Unread tab works across them all, and searching while All inboxes is open looks through every inbox.

Scam emails are the biggest worry

Warnings about scams, in plain English

Every email you open is checked on your PC for the tell-tale signs of a scam: a fake Royal Mail, HMRC or bank sender, a link that goes somewhere other than it says, a dangerous attachment, or pressure words like “within 24 hours”. It tells you exactly why. While Techies One is running, even with its window minimised or closed, obvious scams go to a Scams folder and the sender is blocked; anyone you’ve emailed before is never moved to Scams, though you’re told to be careful if something looks wrong. Like any checker it can get things wrong, so if in doubt, ask us.

“Is this really from them?”

It never tells you an email is safe

When the check finds no warning signs, a plain grey line says only that: Techies One’s check did not flag the email, and that doesn’t prove it is safe. If all an email has in its favour is that you’ve written to that person before (their account could have been taken over), an amber Be careful box says so, with Move to Scams. If an email asks for money, bank details or a code, check by phone first. Pictures from the internet stay hidden until you choose to show them, so senders can’t tell when you’ve opened their email.

Settings, ports and passwords put people off

Type your address; it does the rest

Techies One knows the settings for the big UK providers, so there are no server names or port numbers to look up. It tells you which password it needs (BT’s normal one, or a Gmail app password) and checks everything works before it saves. Moving from an old program? Look for my old email settings adds the addresses it used; you type each password once, because Techies One never reads saved passwords.

“Where’s the button to make a folder?”

Your own email folders, as in Outlook

Select New folder under your email address to make a folder for the club, the house or the family. The … button beside a folder makes one inside it, renames it or deletes it (its emails go to Deleted first), and Move to puts an email into any of them, in Simple view too. The folders are made at your email provider, so a phone reading the same address sees them as well.

Years of newsletters and junk

Clean up your inbox in minutes

Clean up lists everyone who fills your inbox, biggest first: mailing lists, emails you never open, senders gone quiet for over a year. Unsubscribe from genuine newsletters in one press (it refuses on anything suspicious, because that would tell a scammer your address works), delete or archive all of a sender’s emails at once, or send a whole company to Junk from now on. Deleted email goes to Deleted, so nothing is lost by mistake.

Years of emails stuck in an old program

Bring in old emails from Outlook, Windows Live Mail and Thunderbird

Bring in old emails copies emails, contacts and calendars from an Outlook .pst or .ost file, Windows Live Mail, Windows Mail, Thunderbird, a Google Takeout download or a folder of saved emails, and from Outlook Express files too (that one is still experimental). Look for them on this PC searches the usual places for you. They go into a mailbox under On this PC, by your inboxes, kept on your computer rather than using your email company’s space, and the files they came from are never changed.

Christmas cards and a pile of envelopes to write

Envelopes, labels and your Christmas card list

Tick the people, then press Envelopes (C6, DL, C5 or any size) or Labels (the usual A4 sheets of sticky labels, starting at whichever label is next). Big buttons and big text when you want them

// WHY YOUR OLD EMAIL PROGRAM STOPPED

What happened to Windows Mail, Outlook Express and Windows Live Mail?

Each was Microsoft’s free email program for its time, and each has ended. For most people the email itself is still with their email provider, waiting for a program that can open it. The exception is an old program set to the “POP” setting, which kept email only on that PC; Techies One can bring those emails in from the old program’s files.

A warning about “Outlook Express for Windows 11” downloads. Microsoft no longer offers Outlook Express or Windows Live Mail anywhere, so a website offering them is not Microsoft, and old copies get no security fixes. Many people also call Outlook.com or the new Outlook “Outlook Express”: if that’s the one you mean, it is still going, and our Outlook help covers it.

Where does your email actually live?

Why your email is usually still there when the old program stops, and when it isn’t.

An animated diagram with a spoken guide and captions.

What the film says

Where does your email actually live? Usually, not on your computer. It’s kept by your email company, like BT, Sky or Gmail. Your computer and your phone each show you a copy of the same email. So if your old email program stops working, your email is still there. A new program shows it again. But some older programs, like Outlook Express, were often set up to move email onto one computer. Then it’s only on that PC. Your phone can’t see it, and if the PC fails, it can be lost. Not sure which you have? Ring us before you change anything.

// SAFE TO USE

Is it safe to put my email password into it?

Your email, files and passwords

  • Your email goes straight between your PC and your email provider. It never passes through 365 Techies.
  • Passwords are locked with Windows’ own protection, so they only open for you, on that PC.
  • For Outlook.com, Hotmail and OneDrive you sign in on Microsoft’s own page; Techies One never sees that password.
  • Only secure, encrypted connections to your provider are allowed.
  • Files never deletes anything for good without asking: it goes to the Recycle Bin (a USB stick has none, so it asks first). Programs and scripts are never started from Files.
  • A shared link points to the copy in your own OneDrive. 365 Techies never holds your files.
  • Albums never changes, moves or uploads your photo files, never looks for faces, and sends nothing about your photos to us.
  • Old emails you bring in are copied: the files they came from are never changed. Backups never contain your passwords.

What it tells us, and updates

  • To hear about updates it checks in with us when it starts and every few hours. It sends only a random install number, its version, your Windows version and which kinds of provider you use (for example BT or Gmail). Never your addresses, emails, contacts, files or passwords. Details are in our privacy policy.
  • The website sees your internet address when it checks in; we keep only the country, and delete an install we haven’t heard from for a year.
  • Nothing else goes to us unless you press Ask 365 Techies and send the form, which sends the name, phone number and message you type.
  • When you add an address it doesn’t already know, it looks up the settings using only the part after the @ (for example example.co.uk).
  • When you press Dictate, Microsoft turns your speech into words over the internet. If you switch on the optional AI writing helper, the email you ask about goes to Anthropic.
  • The installer and every update are digitally signed by 365 Techies Ltd. Before an update installs, Techies One checks it is genuinely ours, and throws it away if not.
  • It tells you when there’s an update and installs it when you press Update now.

// HONEST LIMITS

What it can’t do (yet)

  • Windows PCs only. There’s no Mac, iPhone, iPad or Android version. Your phone keeps using its own email app, and both stay in step because your email is kept at your provider.
  • It checks new email only while Techies One is running. It looks in every inbox about every 2 minutes, even with its window minimised or closed, to move scams, run your rules and pop up new-email and calendar notes. Close it completely, or switch the PC off, and that stops: your provider’s own spam filter keeps working, and Techies One catches up when you next open it. It can start when Windows starts, if you tick that when you install it.
  • Send later needs your PC on. Emails set to send later wait on your PC and go at that time if it’s on and Techies One is running (it keeps running when you close its window); otherwise they go when you next open it.
  • Search looks through one folder at a time (or, from All inboxes, every inbox at once), including old email, and shows the newest 40 matches. It can’t yet search Sent, Archive and your other folders all together.
  • No Flag button yet. A rule can flag emails as they arrive, but you can’t flag one by hand.
  • Outlook’s .pst files are brought in, not opened in place. Bring in old emails copies their emails, contacts and calendar into a mailbox on this PC. It doesn’t run Outlook add-ins or repair Outlook: it replaces it for everyday email. Outlook Express files are still experimental: if one won’t come in, ask us.
  • No POP. It reads email kept on your provider’s server (IMAP), so new email needs an internet connection. Emails in a mailbox on this PC can be read without one.
  • Gmail, Yahoo, AOL and Virgin Media need an app password, and work addresses on Microsoft 365 usually need an administrator to approve the app and switch on sending (SMTP AUTH) for the mailbox.
  • Google Calendar shows read-only for now (from its private link). Outlook.com calendars sync both ways.
  • Letters is for everyday letters, not everything Word does. Headers and footers, page numbers, text boxes, comments and tracked changes aren’t there: a Word file that has them opens as a copy, says which parts it left out, and your original is never touched.
  • Sums has no charts, and an Excel file with charts, pictures, macros or pivot tables opens as a copy with those parts named and left out. It knows about 90 of Excel’s formulas, the everyday ones.
  • Dictate needs the internet. Where it isn’t available, Windows’ own voice typing (the Windows key and H) still works.
  • WhatsApp opens ready to send: you press Send. Techies One never sends anything in WhatsApp for you.
  • Signing in to a cloud inside Techies One is OneDrive only for now (Dropbox is coming); Google Drive’s and Dropbox’s own apps’ folders on the PC already show in Files. Share a link uses your own OneDrive’s space.
  • iCloud needs Apple’s iCloud for Windows app on the PC (Apple offers no other way in). Moving photos out of iCloud takes them off your iPhone and iPad too, and iCloud keeps them in Recently Deleted for 30 days.
  • Space makes room once the Recycle Bin is emptied. Removed copies go to the Recycle Bin, so they still take up room until you empty it; Space says so.
  • Albums shows photos that are on this PC (and, once you sign in and say yes, the ones in your OneDrive). Photos kept only in Google Photos, Amazon Photos or Facebook can’t be shown; Albums explains how to bring them onto the PC.
  • Albums doesn’t edit photos or recognise faces. It can turn a photo; you type who’s in it. Picasa’s face names and places aren’t brought across, on purpose.
  • Photos from iPhones and some other phones (HEIC) need Microsoft’s add-on from the Microsoft Store before Windows can show them (79p in October 2026). Albums tells you if it’s missing.

// GOOD QUESTIONS

Frequently asked

Is Techies One really free?

Yes. The program costs nothing, Mail, Albums, Letters, Sums and Files included: no charge, no trial, no adverts and no account to make with us, and you can add as many email addresses as you like. Two optional things can cost money. The AI writing helper is charged by Anthropic for each use, if you switch it on with your own key. And if you’d like us to set it up or sort out a problem, that’s a paid job (remote jobs from £20), and we agree the price with you before we start.

Is Techies One the same as Techies One Mail?

Yes. Techies One Mail grew Letters, Sums, Files and Albums, so the whole program is now called Techies One, and Mail is one part of it. An update keeps your email, your saved passwords and your settings.

Do I need Word or Excel?

No. Letters saves ordinary Word files and Sums saves ordinary Excel files, so they also open in Word and Excel on any computer, and Word and Excel files people send you open in Techies One. Letters and Sums are for everyday letters, CVs, budgets and accounts: if you need headers and footers, charts or macros, keep Word and Excel for those.

Is there a replacement for Picasa?

Yes: Albums, in Techies One, free for Windows 10 and 11. Google stopped making Picasa in 2016. Albums shows all your photos in the order they were taken, with slideshows, albums, captions and memories, and it brings across what you did in Picasa: stars become favourites, captions come with the photos and Picasa 3.9 albums become My albums. It doesn’t edit photos or recognise faces: you type who’s in a photo, and the names stay on your PC.

Can I open my old Outlook .pst file or Windows Live Mail emails?

Yes, by bringing them in. In Mail, select Bring in old emails: Techies One copies the emails, contacts and calendar from an Outlook .pst or .ost file, Windows Live Mail, Windows Mail or Thunderbird (and, still experimentally, Outlook Express) into a mailbox under On this PC. The files they came from aren’t changed. If it can’t read one, ask us.

Can I make my own email folders?

Yes. Select New folder under your email address, and use Move to to put emails into it. The folders are made at your email provider, so your phone sees them too.

Can it print envelopes and address labels?

Yes. Add postal addresses in Contacts, tick the people, and press Envelopes or Labels: it prints C6, DL, C5 or other envelopes, or the usual A4 sheets of address labels, and keeps a Christmas card list year by year.

Does Dictate need the internet?

Yes. Microsoft turns your speech into words over the internet, as Windows’ own voice typing does, and only while you have Dictate switched on. Windows may ask once whether Techies One can use the microphone. If Dictate isn’t available, the Windows key and H starts Windows’ own voice typing.

Can it get my photos out of iCloud?

Yes, if Apple’s iCloud for Windows app is on the PC: your iCloud Drive and iCloud Photos then show in Files. Move out of iCloud moves photos and videos to the PC or a USB stick and says how much iCloud space that frees. It takes them off iCloud on your iPhone and iPad too, and iCloud keeps them in Recently Deleted for 30 days. Copying instead of moving leaves iCloud as it was.

Does it work with Dropbox?

If the Dropbox app is on your PC, its folder shows in Files like any other, so you can see, copy and move your Dropbox files. Signing in to Dropbox inside Techies One, without the Dropbox app, is coming; OneDrive works that way now.

What replaced Windows Mail in Windows 11?

Microsoft ended support for its Mail and Calendar app on 31 December 2024 and now offers the new Outlook, which is free but shows adverts unless you pay for Microsoft 365. Techies One is a free alternative without adverts.

Can I still use Outlook Express on Windows 11?

No. Outlook Express came with Windows XP and ended in 2014, and Microsoft doesn’t offer it any more. Websites offering it for Windows 11 are not Microsoft and the copies get no security fixes. Techies One is a modern, free replacement.

Can I still use Windows Live Mail with Windows 11?

Not safely. Support ended in January 2017, so it gets no security fixes; it no longer works with Hotmail or Outlook.com; and it can’t use the modern sign-in Outlook.com and Hotmail now need. Microsoft no longer offers it, so download sites offering it are not Microsoft.

Is there a free alternative to Outlook?

Yes: Techies One, Thunderbird and the free versions of eM Client and Mailbird all work on Windows. Techies One explains each scam warning in plain English, blocks a sender in one press for free, and comes from a UK firm you can ring. The comparison table above shows the limits of each.

Can I use email on my PC without paying for Microsoft 365?

Yes. You don’t need Microsoft 365 to use BT, Sky, Gmail or Outlook.com email on a PC. Techies One is free and has no adverts. Keep Microsoft 365 if you use Word, Excel or OneDrive backup.

Does it work with BT, Sky, Virgin Media and Plusnet email?

Yes, with two exceptions: BT Basic email, which BT only allows on bt.com, and Virgin Media mailboxes that Virgin won’t give an app password for (often a second mailbox). BT and Sky use your normal password, Plusnet your mailbox password, and Virgin Media an app password from the My Virgin Media app.

What is the easiest email program for older people?

Look for big, clear buttons, large text and warnings about scams. Techies One has a Simple view with bigger buttons, three text sizes, read-aloud, Help on every screen and an Ask 365 Techies button that asks a real person at 365 Techies to ring you back (any paid help is priced before we start).

How do I block someone sending me emails?

Open one of their emails and press Block sender. From then on, while Techies One is running (even with its window minimised or closed), anything new they send is moved to your Scams folder, and your phone sees it there too. It doesn’t stop their emails reaching your provider, so a new one can show on your phone until Techies One next checks. You can unblock them from the address book at any time.

Can I see all my email addresses in one inbox?

Yes. Once you’ve added a second address, All inboxes appears at the top of the folder list: the email from every address in one list, newest first, each marked with the address it came to. When you reply, it’s sent from the address the email went to. It’s free, with no limit on how many addresses you add.

How do I free up space on my PC safely?

In Techies One, open Files and then Space. It shows how full each drive is and what’s using the room, and finds duplicate photos and videos and the biggest files. Removed copies go to the Recycle Bin, so you can get them back, and it never removes the last copy of anything. The room comes back when you empty the Recycle Bin.

Will I lose my old emails if I switch?

Usually not. If your old program used the normal setting (IMAP), your emails are kept with your email provider, so they appear in Techies One, folders and all, as soon as you add your address. Outlook Express and Windows Live Mail were often set to the older POP setting instead, which kept email only on the old PC. Then Bring in old emails can copy them from the old program’s files into a mailbox on this PC (Outlook Express files are still experimental). Keep the old program or PC until they’re in, and ask us if you’re not sure.

Is it safe to put my email password into it?

Yes. Passwords are locked with Windows’ own protection on your PC and never sent to us, and your email goes straight between your PC and your provider. The program is digitally signed by 365 Techies Ltd.

Does it work on a Mac, iPad or phone?

No, it is for Windows 10 and 11 (64-bit) PCs only. Your phone or tablet keeps using its own email app, and everything stays in step because your email is kept at your provider.
