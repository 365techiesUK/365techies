/02 — HONEST COMPARISON

VRM is excellent. Here’s what custom adds.

Let’s be straight: Victron’s VRM portal is polished, free, and keeps getting better — live data every couple of seconds while you’re watching, a rearrangeable Advanced tab with a custom widget builder, alarms to your phone, even remote control of your system. A custom dashboard doesn’t replace VRM — it sits alongside it (read-only, via Victron’s own API) and does the things VRM deliberately doesn’t.

// HOW IT WORKS

Built on Victron’s open ecosystem — never replacing it

Your VRM stays the system of record; we add the layer that makes the data make sense — the same way we built our own van’s live dashboard.

01 · Read your VRM, safely

A small server-side proxy reads Victron’s official read-only VRM API — your login never touches the browser, and nothing can be changed from the dashboard.

02 · Genuinely real-time

A minimal MQTT client over TLS streams live, per-second data to the screen — well beyond VRM’s roughly 15-minute logging interval.

03 · Show what matters

A live grid-savings meter, a battery passport, a self-calibrating 7-day solar forecast, several sites on one screen, Grafana panels — hosted by us or on your own subdomain.

/07 — ON YOUR OWN WEBSITE

Your brand, your domain — your dashboard can live on your own website

Everywhere else on this page we say the same thing: we build it, we host it, we look after it, and you get a link. That stays the default, and for most owners it’s the easy answer. But if you run a business — an installer, a hire fleet, a marina, a holiday site — there’s a second route worth knowing about: your dashboard can live on your own website, ideally on a fresh subdomain such as monitor.yourcompany.co.uk or dash.yourcompany.co.uk, linked straight into your main site’s navigation.

Your part is three small jobs, none of them technical heroics. One: create a VRM access token in your own VRM account — a two-minute job, and we send a plain-English walkthrough. We recommend creating it under a ‘monitor only’ VRM user (which VRM genuinely supports, per site), so the token can only ever read data. Two: add the subdomain in your hosting control panel — one click in cPanel, Site Tools, Plesk and the like. Three: give us FTP or SFTP access scoped to just that subdomain’s folder. We do everything else — design, build and deployment.

Why bother? Because the ownership sums change:

  • Your address, end to end. Customers click from your menu to monitor.yourcompany.co.uk and back without ever leaving your domain — the same your-brand-only finish as our white-label monitoring, on hosting you already pay for.
  • The token is yours and stays server-side. It sits in one server-side file the dashboard reads; it never appears in the page code and is never visible to visitors — and you can revoke it in VRM whenever you like.
  • Our access is fenced in. The FTP or SFTP login only reaches the new subdomain’s folder — we never need, and never ask for, the rest of your site. Revoke that too, any time.
  • Room to grow. Extra pages, extra dashboards and extra features can be added there over time as your needs change.
  • Long-term ownership. The dashboard sits on your hosting, at your domain — if our arrangement ever ended, the files would still be sitting on your server.

One honest requirement: the dashboard needs a small server-side relay to keep that token out of the page — a small PHP file, which nearly all ordinary shared hosting runs as standard. If yours can’t, nothing is lost: we simply host it for you instead, exactly as described above.

And this isn’t theory — it’s how we run our own. The live 365 Crafter van dashboards further up this page work on precisely this architecture: a static page, one server-side file holding a read-only token, and a small relay. If all you want is a simple widget on an existing page, our guide to embedding VRM on your website may be enough; if you want the full custom dashboard under your own roof, this is the route. Tell us which way you lean when you book a free demo — either answer is fine by us.

/10 — GPS & ROUTE TRACKING

Moving system? Put it on a live map — and keep every route

If your Victron system moves, it can carry GPS — a third-party USB receiver on the Cerbo GX (around £18 from UK retailers at the time of writing), the GX LTE 4G with its optional GPS antenna, or a boat’s existing NMEA 2000 GPS fed in over VE.Can. Credit where it’s due: once a fix is flowing, Victron’s own VRM portal already gives you a map of all your installations, a drawable geofence that alarms on exit and on signal loss, and a downloadable position history — those are Victron’s features, and they’re good.

What we add is the layer that makes it yours:

  • A live moving map, on your branded dashboard. Position streams in over the same feed that drives the live demos above — near-live, typically updating every second or two on the move.
  • Route history that stays yours. VRM keeps its detailed data for at least six months, not forever; our dashboards archive every track themselves, so your routes never expire.
  • Blackspots that heal. The GX buffers its logs on board and uploads them when the connection returns — a signal gap pauses the live pin, never the route.
  • One van or a whole fleet. The same build tracks a single camper, a boat, or every vehicle in a hire fleet on one map.

And one honest sentence for anyone thinking ‘theft tracker’: it isn’t one — it runs off the very battery bank it monitors, isn’t concealed and isn’t insurer-recognised, so a dedicated tracker still owns that job, with VRM’s geofence and signal-loss alarm the honest next-best thing. For the hardware choices, the gotchas and the limits, read the full GPS tracking guide — or book a free demo and we’ll talk it through.

  • GPS hardware from around £18 at retail
  • Live moving map on your branded dashboard
  • Route history archived beyond VRM’s detail window
  • Geofence and signal-loss alarms built into VRM
  • Signal blackspots backfill — routes stay complete
  • Works for vans, boats and hire fleets

// GOOD QUESTIONS

Frequently asked

Why does my Victron VRM dashboard only update every 15 minutes?

Because VRM’s stored data updates at your GX device’s logging interval — 15 minutes by default (you can shorten it in the device settings, at the cost of more data use). That’s ideal for history and diagnostics, but it’s why an embedded VRM view never feels live. Genuinely per-second dashboards — like the van demos on this page — come from Victron’s MQTT stream, the same live feed the official apps use, which is exactly what our custom dashboards read alongside the official API.

Can you customise the Victron VRM dashboard?

Not the VRM portal itself — Victron’s Advanced Dashboard offers configurable widgets, but the portal’s layout, branding and structure are fixed. What we build instead is a custom dashboard alongside VRM: your layout, your branding, your KPIs, reading the same live data through Victron’s official interfaces. VRM stays untouched as the system of record.

Is there any way to customise the VRM portal?

Within VRM you can rearrange the Advanced Dashboard’s widgets and choose which values show — and for many owners that’s enough. When people ask this, they usually want their own branding, a single screen for several sites, financial KPIs, or a customer-facing view — none of which VRM is designed to do. That’s exactly the gap our custom dashboards fill, without replacing VRM.

Does this replace the VRM portal?

No — and it shouldn’t. VRM stays your system of record, with its alarms, history and remote console. Your custom dashboard reads the same data through Victron’s official API and presents it your way, alongside VRM.

Do I need any new hardware?

Usually not. If your system already reports to VRM (via a GX device such as a Cerbo GX), we can build from that today using a read-only API token. No site visit needed — anywhere in the world.

Is it safe? Can a dashboard change my system?

We build with a read-only VRM access token, kept server-side — the dashboard can display your system but never control or change it. Optional password protection on the page itself if you want it private.

Can you put several installations on one screen?

Yes — that’s one of the main reasons people come to us. Any number of sites, mixed hardware, one live page; VRM’s own multi-site tools are a list view and a fleet dashboard that needs ten or more sites.

Can you include non-Victron equipment?

Usually, yes — most modern kit exposes an API or feed we can read (other solar brands, sensors, weather, business data). Tell us what you run and we’ll confirm feasibility honestly before you commit.

Can the dashboard live on my own website?

Yes. We host by default, but if you’d rather have it at your own address — say monitor.yourcompany.co.uk — you create a read-only VRM access token (two minutes; we send a plain-English walkthrough), add the subdomain in your hosting panel, and give us FTP or SFTP access to just that folder. The token sits in one server-side file, never appears in the page code, and you can revoke it — or our access — whenever you like. Your host just needs to run a small PHP relay, which nearly all ordinary shared hosting supports; if yours can’t, we simply host it for you instead. It’s the same architecture our own live van dashboards on this page run on.

What does a custom dashboard cost?

Every dashboard is different, so we don’t publish a one-size price — you get a free demo and a fixed written quote up front, including hosting and support, before any work starts.

Where do you cover?

Worldwide. The service is remote by design — we’ve built and supported ours from Dorset, England, and yours works the same wherever the system lives. We work UK office hours and schedule calls to suit your time zone.
