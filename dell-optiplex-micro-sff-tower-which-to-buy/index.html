Dell OptiPlex Micro vs SFF vs Tower — which to buy?

For most offices the SFF (small form factor) is the sweet spot — it slides under a desk yet still takes RAM and SSD upgrades. Choose the Micro if space and silence matter more than expansion, and the Tower only if you need a dedicated graphics card or maximum internal room. All three are built to the same dependable OptiPlex standard, so the right pick comes down to where it lives and what you plug into it — and we supply all three, professionally refurbished, from £510. // THE SHORT ANSWER

Micro, SFF or Tower — the quick verdict

Most people should buy the SFF. It’s the small-form-factor OptiPlex that sits under a monitor or desk, stays quiet, and still lets us add RAM and a bigger SSD later. Step down to the Micro only if desk space and silence really matter; step up to the Tower only if you genuinely need a dedicated graphics card or lots of internal expansion.

  • Micro — tiny, monitor-mountable, near-silent. Great for reception desks, hot-desks and tight spaces. No room for an add-in graphics card.
  • SFF (small form factor) — the office all-rounder. Slim, upgradeable, and it’ll take a low-profile graphics card if you ever need one.
  • Tower — biggest and most expandable. The one to pick when a full-size graphics card, extra drives or maximum future headroom is the point.

All three are built to the same dependable OptiPlex standard, so this is really a question of where the machine lives and what you plug into it — not raw speed. We stock refurbished Dell OptiPlex desktops across Dorset in all three shapes from £545.

// SMALLEST

OptiPlex Micro — tiny, quiet, monitor-mountable

The OptiPlex Micro is the smallest of the family — roughly a litre in size, small enough to strap to the back of a monitor with a VESA bracket or tuck out of sight on a shelf. It runs from an external power brick, like a laptop, and is close to silent in use.

Buy the Micro when:

  • Desk space is tight — reception counters, hot-desks, meeting rooms, kiosks, wall-mounted signage.
  • Noise matters — consulting rooms, studios, quiet offices.
  • The job is everyday work: email, Microsoft 365, web apps, accounts and browsing.

Its limits: the Micro typically uses laptop-style memory and has room for a fast M.2 SSD (sometimes one small extra drive), but there are no internal expansion slots — so you cannot add a dedicated graphics card, capture card or extra internal drives. If that might ever matter, size up to the SFF. Either way we can fit a larger SSD to keep it quick for years.

// THE OFFICE ALL-ROUNDER

OptiPlex SFF — the office sweet spot

The small form factor OptiPlex is the one we sell most, and for good reason: it’s the sensible middle. It’s a slim desktop that stands upright beside a monitor or lies flat under one, tucks under a desk, and has a proper internal power supply — no wall-wart needed.

Why it’s the sweet spot:

  • Takes standard desktop memory, usually with room to add more.
  • Room for a fast M.2 SSD plus an extra 2.5-inch or 3.5-inch drive.
  • Has one or two low-profile (half-height) expansion slots, so it can take a small graphics card, extra ports or a capture card if you ever need one — something the Micro simply can’t.
  • Still compact and quiet enough for any normal office.

For the vast majority of Bournemouth and Poole firms buying office computers, the SFF is the right call. If one ever plays up, our Dell OptiPlex repair service in Poole covers it, and a common F1 fan error is usually a quick fix.

// MAXIMUM EXPANSION

OptiPlex Tower — only if you need a graphics card

The OptiPlex Tower (Dell also calls it the Mini Tower or MT) is the biggest and most expandable body. The reason to choose it is simple: full-height expansion slots. That means it can take a proper, full-size dedicated graphics card — plus more internal drive bays and a larger power supply than the Micro or SFF.

Buy the Tower when:

  • You need a dedicated graphics card — CAD, 3D, video editing, multi-screen design work, or a spot of gaming after hours.
  • You want lots of internal storage or plan to keep adding drives.
  • You value maximum future headroom and easy servicing — there’s simply more room inside to work.

If your needs go beyond even a Tower — a high-end or workstation graphics card, or specialist cards — a custom PC build may suit better, and we’re happy to advise. For most offices, though, a Tower is more machine than you need.

// HOW TO CHOOSE

Match the body to the job (a 3-question guide)

Still deciding? Work through it in this order:

  • Where will it live? If it must hide behind a monitor or fit a tiny space → Micro.
  • Do you need a dedicated graphics card? If yes → Tower (or an SFF with a low-profile card for lighter needs).
  • Neither of the above? Buy the SFF — it handles almost any office job and leaves room to upgrade.

Whichever you choose, every refurbished OptiPlex we sell comes with a brand-new Samsung Pro SSD (5-year guarantee), a clean licensed copy of Windows, and our own 5-year guarantee on top of the Dell warranty that ships with the machine. // GOOD QUESTIONS

Frequently asked

Is a Dell OptiPlex Micro powerful enough for an office?

For everyday office work — email, Microsoft 365, accounts, web apps and browsing — yes, easily. The Micro is built on the same dependable OptiPlex platform as its bigger siblings; the main thing you give up is internal expansion. If you need a dedicated graphics card or lots of extra drives, choose the SFF or Tower instead.

Can you put a graphics card in a Dell OptiPlex SFF?

You can fit a low-profile (half-height) graphics card in an SFF, which is fine for extra display outputs or light graphics work — but not a full-size gaming or workstation card. For a proper full-size graphics card you need the OptiPlex Tower. The Micro can’t take any add-in card at all.

What’s the difference between OptiPlex Micro, SFF and Tower?

It’s mainly size and expansion. Micro is tiny and monitor-mountable but has no expansion slots; SFF is a slim desktop with low-profile slots and room to upgrade RAM and storage; Tower is the largest, with full-height slots for a dedicated graphics card and the most drive bays. Performance is broadly similar — the body is about where it fits and what you can add.

For most Bournemouth offices the SFF is the best value — slim, quiet and upgradeable. Go Micro for reception desks or tight spaces, and Tower only if staff need a dedicated graphics card. Bring us your use case on 01202 775566 and we’ll steer you to the right one, no upsell.
