Laptop won’t turn on — no lights, no charge

A laptop that shows no lights and won’t charge feels like the worst news — but it’s often something simple. Here’s exactly what to check yourself, safely and in order, and when it’s time to let us take a proper look — free diagnosis, no-fix-no-fee, right here in Dorset. Rated 4.9 on Google.

/01 — START HERE

Laptop won’t turn on, no lights, not charging? Do this first

If your laptop won’t turn on, shows no lights and isn’t charging, the fault is almost always one of three things: the charger or cable, the battery, or the power circuitry on the motherboard. Work through the free checks below in order — a surprising number of ‘dead’ laptops are simply stuck in a power state and come back after a proper power drain.

Start with the cheapest, most common cause first. Confirm the charger is actually delivering power, force a hard reset to clear the stuck state, then give a flat battery time to take a charge. If none of that wakes it, the fault is internal and needs a proper bench diagnosis — which we do free, with no-fix-no-fee. A quick way to read the symptom:

  • No lights at all — usually the charger, the charging port or the battery.
  • A light comes on but the screen stays black — more likely the display, memory or board (covered further down).
  • It was fine, then suddenly died — often a failed charger or a deeply flat battery.

/02 — CHECK THE CHARGER

Rule out the charger, cable and socket

Most ‘completely dead’ laptops turn out to have a power-supply problem rather than a broken computer. Chargers fail far more often than motherboards, so rule this out before anything else.

  • Test the wall socket. Plug something you know works — a lamp or phone charger — into the same socket. Try a different socket, and take extension leads out of the test.
  • Look at the charger brick. Many adapters have a small LED on the block or plug. If it stays dark, the charger or its cable may be the culprit.
  • Feel along the cable. Kinks, scorching or a wobbly connector are common failure points, especially where the lead meets the brick and the tip that goes into the laptop.
  • Watch the charging light on the laptop. Many models have a tiny LED beside the power socket that glows amber or white when charging. No light there, with a charger you trust, points to the charging port or battery.
  • Borrow a known-good charger of the correct type and wattage if you can. An underpowered replacement may not wake a flat laptop.

If a different charger brings it to life, you’ve found the problem. Not sure which charger suits your model? We can identify and supply the correct one.

/03 — POWER DRAIN

Force a power drain (hard reset)

Laptops can get stuck in a power state where they look dead but aren’t. A power drain (also called a hard reset) clears the residual charge left in the board and resets the controller that manages power — it’s the single most effective free fix for a no-lights laptop.

  • Unplug the charger and remove any USB sticks, memory cards, docks and external drives.
  • If your battery is removable, flip the laptop over, release the catch and take it out. Many modern laptops have a sealed internal battery — if so, simply leave it in place.
  • Press and hold the power button for 15 to 30 seconds. This drains the capacitors on the board.
  • Refit the battery (if you removed it), reconnect only the charger, then press the power button once.

Some laptops also have a tiny pinhole reset button on the underside — a recessed hole, sometimes marked with a small battery symbol. With the charger unplugged, gently press it with a straightened paperclip for around 15 seconds, then reconnect power. Check your model’s manual before poking any hole, as not every opening is a reset.

/04 — GIVE IT TIME

Let a flat battery take a charge

A battery that has run completely flat can be too low to show any light or power the laptop straight away. It needs a spell on charge before it will respond — so don’t assume ‘no reaction’ means ‘dead’ just yet.

  • Leave it plugged in and untouched for at least 30 minutes — a deeply discharged battery may need a couple of hours before it holds enough charge to boot.
  • Use the original charger where possible, plugged straight into the wall rather than a multi-way extension.
  • Watch for any change — a charging light appearing, a faint fan whir or a warm patch near the charging port all suggest power is now getting in.
  • Then try the power button again after that charging period, not before.

If, after a good charge and a power drain, there is still absolutely nothing — no lights, no sound, no warmth — the fault is very likely inside the laptop. That’s the point to stop and get it looked at properly, before trial-and-error risks making things worse. Our repair-or-replace advisor can help you weigh up what to do next.

/05 — SIGNS OF LIFE

Is it truly dead, or on with a black screen?

Before you write the laptop off, it’s worth telling the difference between ‘truly dead’ and ‘powered on, but showing nothing’ — because they are very different repairs.

  • Listen and feel. With the charger connected, press power and put your ear close. A fan spinning, a single beep, or the drive ticking means the laptop is powering up, even if the screen stays black.
  • Look for a faint glow. In a dark room, shine a torch at an angle across the screen. If you can just make out a desktop, the backlight has failed rather than the whole laptop.
  • Connect an external monitor or TV using an HDMI cable. If a picture appears there but the laptop’s own screen stays black, the problem is the display or its cable — not the computer — and a screen repair usually sorts it.

If the fans spin but nothing shows on the laptop or an external screen, that points to a deeper fault — memory, graphics or the board itself — which needs opening up to diagnose. If there’s no reaction whatsoever, you’re back to a charger, port or power fault.

/06 — WHEN TO CALL US

When it needs a proper bench repair

If the free checks haven’t worked, the fault is one that can’t safely be fixed at the kitchen table. The usual culprits behind a no-lights, no-charge laptop are a failed charging port (DC jack), a dead or faulty battery, or a power fault on the motherboard — and telling them apart takes proper test gear on the bench.

Here’s how we handle it. We test the charger and its output, check the charging port and battery, and trace where the power actually stops. Then we tell you honestly what’s wrong and what it will cost — before any work goes ahead.

  • Free diagnosis — we find the fault and quote before you commit to anything.
  • No fix, no fee — if we can’t sort it, you don’t pay for the attempt.
  • Collection available across Bournemouth, Poole, Christchurch and the wider Dorset area, so a dead laptop needn’t leave the house.

Some board-level faults simply aren’t economical to repair on an older machine. Where that’s the case we’ll say so plainly, help you rescue your files first, and — if you’d rather replace it — we offer refurbished business-grade laptops from £510.

/07 — YOUR FILES

A dead laptop rarely means lost files

The biggest worry with a dead laptop is usually the photos, documents and memories on it — not the machine itself. The good news: a laptop that won’t power on very rarely means the data is gone.

In most cases the storage drive is perfectly healthy; it’s the power, the charging port or the screen that has failed, not the drive. We can remove the drive, read your files on our own equipment and copy them somewhere safe — even when the laptop will never switch on again.

  • Don’t keep hammering the power button in the hope it springs back — if a drive is failing, repeated attempts can make things worse.
  • Bring it in as it is. There’s no need to do anything clever first; we’d rather see it untouched.

If the drive itself has failed, that’s a specialist job — our data recovery service covers exactly that. Either way, getting your files back is usually the first thing we do, before any decision about repairing or replacing the laptop.

// GOOD QUESTIONS

Frequently asked

Why won’t my laptop turn on even though it’s plugged in?

Being plugged in doesn’t guarantee power is actually reaching the laptop. The charger, its cable or the charging port can fail, or the battery may be too flat to respond. It can also be stuck in a power state that a hard reset clears. Work through the charger checks and a power drain first — if there’s still no light, the charging port or board is the likely cause.

How long should I charge a dead laptop before trying again?

Give it at least 30 minutes on the original charger, plugged straight into the wall. A battery that has run completely flat can need a couple of hours before it holds enough charge to boot. Watch for a charging light, a warm patch near the port or a faint fan — any of those means power is getting in. If nothing changes after a good charge, the fault is likely internal.

What is a power drain and is it safe to do?

A power drain, or hard reset, clears leftover charge in the laptop and resets the controller that manages power. It’s safe and often the fix for a no-lights laptop. Unplug the charger, remove any USB devices, take out the battery if it’s removable, then hold the power button for 15 to 30 seconds. Refit the battery, reconnect the charger and press power. If your battery is sealed inside, leave it in and hold power for 15 seconds.

My laptop has a light but the screen stays black — is that the same fault?

Not quite — and that’s good news. If a light or fan comes on, the laptop is getting power, so the problem is usually the screen, the ribbon cable, the memory or the graphics rather than the power system. Try connecting an external monitor by HDMI: a picture there points to a screen fault we can repair. No picture anywhere suggests a deeper board issue.

Will I lose my photos and files if the laptop is dead?

Usually not. A laptop that won’t power on rarely means the files are lost — far more often it’s the power, port or screen that has failed while the storage drive is fine. We can take the drive out, read it on our own equipment and copy your files somewhere safe even if the laptop never switches on again. If the drive itself has failed, our data recovery service handles that.

Can you always bring a dead laptop back to life?

We’re honest about this: no. A worn-out charger or a tired battery is often a straightforward fix, but some faults — particularly the charging port or a power problem on the motherboard — need a proper bench diagnosis, and on an older laptop they aren’t always economical to put right. We diagnose the fault, tell you plainly what it would take and what it would cost, and if it isn’t worth mending we’ll say so rather than run up a bill — helping you rescue your files first and weigh up whether a repair or a replacement makes more sense.
