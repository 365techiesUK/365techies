// THE TOP TIER

What the Latitude 7000 tier actually is

The Latitude 7000 series is Dell’s premium business laptop tier — the machines large firms buy for directors and frequent travellers, sitting above the budget 3000 and mainstream 5000 lines. The headline difference is weight: most 7000s weigh around 1.2 kg, and the featherweight 7330 Ultralight is just 0.967 kg — against roughly 1.6 kg or more for an equivalent 5000-series machine. Every model also carries two Thunderbolt 4 / USB-C ports, so one cable handles charging, monitors and docking.

Here’s the part that surprises people: bought refurbished, a 7000 often costs less than a newer 5000. These were expensive fleet laptops bought in volume, and when the corporate leases end they reach the used market in large numbers — so yesterday’s premium compact becomes today’s quiet bargain.

Two honest caveats before you fall for one: there’s no Ethernet port on any model (a USB-C adapter or dock sorts that in seconds), and the memory can never be upgraded — more on that below, because it’s the single rule that governs the whole series.

// READ THIS FIRST

The soldered-RAM rule — the one thing you must get right

Here is the single most important 7000-series buying rule: the RAM is soldered to the motherboard on every model, in every configuration. There are no memory slots anywhere in the range. The RAM a machine has on the day you buy it is the RAM it keeps for ever.

That’s exactly why we stock 16GB as standard and steer customers away from 8GB units entirely. An 8GB 7000 can look like a bargain online, but it can never grow — and 8GB is already tight for Windows 11 with a browser full of tabs. A 16GB machine stays comfortable for years; 32GB units exist for heavy multitaskers, and that’s the maximum across the whole series.

Compare that with the Latitude 5000 series, where the memory upgrades in the normal way. If you want a laptop you can open up and expand later, buy a 5000. If you want the lightest machine and get the spec right on day one, the 7000 is a lovely thing to live with. We’ll always tell you honestly which side of that line you’re on before you spend a penny.

// PROCESSOR ADVICE

Why a 12th-gen i5 beats an 11th-gen i7

The 2021 models (7320 and 7420) use 11th-gen Intel chips with four cores. The 2022 models (7330 and 7430) jump to 12th-gen chips with ten cores — and that upends the usual badge logic. A 12th-gen i5 comfortably outruns even an 11th-gen i7 in multi-core work, so don’t pay extra for an older i7 sticker: the newer i5 is the faster laptop.

Our plain-English steer:

  • Email, web, Office, video calls — an 11th-gen 7320 or 7420 is genuinely quick and the best value in the range.
  • Most buyers’ sweet spot — the 12th-gen i5 in the 7330 or 7430: ten cores, excellent battery life, and a big real-world speed-up over the 2021 machines.
  • Power users — the 7430 also shipped with faster 28-watt P-series chips, and the 13th-gen 7440 tops the whole range for outright pace; ask and we’ll quote what’s in stock.

One honest note: batteries are a wear item on any refurbished laptop, and the smaller base batteries give the shortest runtimes — we test every unit and tell you exactly what it carries.

// STORAGE

A new Samsung 990 PRO in every one — with honest speed notes

Every 7000 we sell is fitted with a brand-new Samsung 990 PRO NVMe SSD — 1TB as standard, covered by Samsung’s own 5-year guarantee. Want more room? 2TB is +£185 and 4TB is +£420 at fitting time.

Now the honesty bit, because the models differ:

  • 7330 and 7430 — the drive slot is PCIe Gen4, so the 990 PRO runs at its full speed.
  • 7320 and 7420 — the slot is PCIe Gen3, so the drive caps at around 3.5 GB/s. That’s still an enormous real-world upgrade over the tired original drives these arrive with — and we fit the PRO for its endurance and that 5-year Samsung guarantee, not the headline speed figure.
  • 7440, and the featherweight 7330 Ultralight — these take only the small M.2 2230 drive size, which Samsung’s retail PRO drives don’t come in. We fit a quality NVMe drive of the equivalent class instead, and we’ll tell you exactly which drive is going in before you buy.

Whichever model you choose, the old drive is securely wiped, Windows 11 goes on clean and licensed, and moving your files across is included in the price — never an extra.

A refurbished Latitude 7000 at the same money buys a machine that sold as premium business kit when new — genuinely light, with business-grade ports, security hardware and a new 1TB Samsung 990 PRO fitted by us.

What you get from us that a marketplace seller won’t give you:

  • Our 5-year guarantee on the laptop, on top of Samsung’s 5-year guarantee on the drive.

// STRAIGHT ANSWER

Why do computers cost more than last year?

In short: the memory chips inside every computer have shot up in price. AI data centres are buying storage and memory at a scale the industry has never seen, and that demand has pushed up the price of the NAND flash chips inside every SSD — for everyone, on every brand, new and refurbished alike.

We fit a brand-new 1TB Samsung 990 PRO in every machine we sell rather than a tired old drive — and that one part now costs around £230 at UK retailers. We’d rather explain the price honestly than quietly fit a cheaper, slower drive.

The upside: a refurbished business-grade Dell is the best answer to rising prices — the chassis, screen and board are already made, so your money goes on the parts that matter. The full story is on our why computer prices have gone up page.

// GOOD QUESTIONS

Frequently asked

Why is a refurbished Latitude 7000 sometimes cheaper than a 5000?

Supply. The 7000 was the premium tier big companies bought in volume for senior staff, and when those fleet leases end the machines reach the used market in large numbers. An older premium compact is often more plentiful than a newer mid-range 5000 — so the lighter, nicer laptop can carry the smaller price tag. Our series explainer covers the full picture.

Can I add more RAM to a Latitude 7000 later?

No — and this is the most important thing to know about the series. The RAM is soldered on every 7000 model, with no memory slots at all, so the RAM it has is the RAM it keeps for ever. That’s why we stock 16GB as standard and won’t recommend 8GB units. The series maximum is 32GB, factory-fitted only.

Is 16GB enough?

For almost everyone, yes — comfortable for Windows 11, Office, dozens of browser tabs and video calls, for years to come. Because it can never be upgraded, 16GB is the sensible minimum; if you run heavyweight multitasking all day, ask us about 32GB units when they’re in stock.

Should I pay more for an i7?

Not automatically. The 11th-gen models (7320/7420) are all four-core chips, and a 12th-gen i5 (7330/7430) with ten cores comfortably beats an 11th-gen i7 in multi-core work. Pay for the newer generation, not the badge — we’ll tell you straight which chip in stock is actually the faster one.

Does the Samsung 990 PRO run at full speed in every model?

Honestly, no. In the 7330 and 7430 the slot is PCIe Gen4, so it runs flat out. In the 7320 and 7420 the slot is Gen3, so the drive caps at around 3.5 GB/s — still a huge real-world upgrade, and the PRO’s endurance and Samsung 5-year guarantee are the reason we fit it regardless.

Why don’t you publish a price for the Latitude 7440?

The 7440 takes only the small M.2 2230 drive size — Samsung’s retail PRO drives are the longer 2280 shape and physically don’t fit. We fit a quality NVMe drive of the equivalent class and quote each 7440 individually, so the price reflects the exact unit and drive. Get in touch for a quote.

What do grades A, B and C mean?

Looks only — nothing else. Grade A is close to pristine, B has light signs of use, C shows honest wear. Every grade is securely wiped, fully tested and working, gets the same new Samsung SSD and the same 365 Techies 5-year guarantee. The grade changes the price, never the reliability.

What’s included in the price, and do you deliver?

16GB RAM, a new 1TB Samsung 990 PRO, Windows 11 Home or Pro, professionally set up, full testing, setup and your data moved across — all included, no extras at checkout. Collect from us in Kinson, Bournemouth BH10 7LH (Mon–Fri 9–5) or we deliver UK-wide. See the whole range at our refurbished Dell shop.

Why are refurbished laptops more expensive than they used to be?

Because the memory chips inside every computer have risen sharply in price — AI data centres are buying storage and memory at unprecedented scale, which has pushed up the cost of the SSDs we fit. The brand-new 1TB Samsung 990 PRO in every machine now costs around £230 on its own. Refurbished business-grade Dell remains the best-value response: the full story is on our why computer prices have gone up page.
