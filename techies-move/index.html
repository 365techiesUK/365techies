Techies Move: the free way to move everything from your old PC to your new one

Techies Move is a free program for Windows 11 and 10 from 365 Techies, the family-run IT firm in Bournemouth. It copies your photos, documents, desktop, music, videos and downloads, your favourites from Edge, Chrome or Firefox, your Outlook email files and signatures and your desktop picture onto a USB stick or external hard drive, then puts them all in the right places on the new PC. It tells you what size of USB stick to buy, lists the passwords you’ll need before you switch the old PC off, and afterwards can keep the stick as your backup. Nothing is deleted, nothing on the new PC is written over, and nothing goes on the internet.

// DOWNLOAD · FREE · WINDOWS 10 & 11

Get Techies Move

The program is free for everyone, at home or at work: no trial, no account to make with us, no adverts and no limit on how much it moves. You’ll need a USB stick or external hard drive with enough room; Techies Move tells you how much.

Download Techies Move (Windows) Rather we did the move? 01202 775566

FOR WINDOWS 10 AND 11 (64-BIT) · VERSION 0.2.0 · 12 MB · DIGITALLY SIGNED BY 365 TECHIES LTD · NOT FOR MAC, IPHONE OR ANDROID

  1. 1. Download it on both computers. Press the Download button on the old PC, and again on the new one. The file is called TechiesMove-Setup-0.2.0.exe and is signed by 365 Techies Ltd. It installs in a few seconds and needs no administrator password.
  2. 2. On the old PC, plug in a USB stick and pack up. Choose This is my OLD computer. Techies Move counts what you have and says what size of stick you need. Choose the stick, untick anything you don’t want, and press Start copying. Your old PC isn’t changed.
  3. 3. On the new PC, put it back. Plug the stick into the new PC, open Techies Move, choose This is my NEW computer and press Put it on this PC. Your pictures go into Pictures, your documents into Documents, and so on, and it shows you what to do next for your favourites, Chrome and email.
What will Windows say when I open it?

Usually nothing: it’s digitally signed by 365 Techies Ltd. But a brand-new program can get a warning or two while Windows gets to know it. When downloading, Edge may say the file “isn’t commonly downloaded”: press the three dots next to it, choose Keep, then Show more and Keep anyway. When you open it, you may see a blue “Windows protected your PC” box: choose More info, check that the Publisher line says 365 Techies Ltd, then choose Run anyway. If the publisher says anything else, don’t run it, and ring us.

Does it need the internet?

Only to download it. The move itself goes through the USB stick, so the two PCs don’t need to be connected to each other, or to anything else, and your files never go online.

// HOW A MOVE WORKS, WITH PICTURES

How to move to a new computer with Techies Move

The pictures are the real program in a fresh copy of Windows 11, moving a made-up person’s files: “Jean”, her laptop and her new PC. A test drive stands in for the USB stick.

“What size of USB stick do I need?”

It works out the size for you, then checks your stick has room

On the old PC, Techies Move counts everything in your folders and tells you straight away, for example “This needs 41 GB, so a 64 GB USB stick or bigger is enough”, and a size up if you’d like to keep the stick as your backup afterwards. Plug a stick in and each one says Room for all of it, or how much more room it needs. Older sticks formatted as FAT32 can’t take a single file of 4 GB or more (a long home video, say), and it warns you about that too.

The things that aren’t in your folders

Favourites, email, your desktop picture and lists of what’s installed

Tick what to take: your favourites from every browser on the PC (Edge, Chrome, Brave and Firefox, including the favourites Chrome keeps in your Google account), Outlook data files (where the emails of older POP accounts live), your email signatures and a list of your email accounts and their settings, your desktop picture, and lists of your programs, printers and Wi-Fi networks. Got photos in another folder, or on a second drive? Press Add another folder.

The thing that catches most people out

Your passwords: checked while the old PC still works

Techies Move never reads or copies passwords. Instead it lists every email address on the PC (from Outlook, the new Outlook and Thunderbird) and every browser you’re signed in to, so you can check you know each password before the old PC goes. Each email address has a Check the password works button that opens your provider’s webmail, and plain advice for BT, Sky, Virgin Media, TalkTalk, Plusnet, Gmail, Outlook.com and more. For Chrome it tells you whether your passwords are already saved in your Google account, and if not, how to take them.

On the new computer

Put it all on the new PC, in the right places

Plug the stick into the new PC and press Put it on this PC. Pictures go into Pictures, documents into Documents, and a folder you added goes into Documents in a folder named after the old PC. If the new PC already has a different file with the same name, both are kept: the old PC’s gets “(from old PC)” on its name. Files keep their dates, so your photos stay in order.

Afterwards

Then it shows you what to do next

When the copying’s done, Techies Move shows the next steps for what can’t simply be copied: how to add your favourites to Edge or Chrome (with a button to the right page), which Google account to sign in to Chrome with (and a button to install Chrome if it isn’t on the new PC), how to set up each email address in Outlook with its settings, and the programs Windows can install again for you, ticked one by one.

“Will my emails come across?”

Your email, set up again in minutes

Most email today (BT, Sky, Gmail, Outlook.com, Virgin Media and more) is kept by your email company, so it all comes back as soon as you add your address in Outlook on the new PC. Techies Move lists each address with what to know (BT has no app passwords; Virgin Media needs an app password, and making a new one stops the old one working on your phone) and the settings, in case Outlook asks. Older POP accounts keep their emails in Outlook files on the PC: those are copied across and opened in Outlook on the new PC. Classic Outlook’s signatures go straight back where it looks for them.

“What do I do with the stick now?”

Keep the stick as your backup

The stick already holds a copy of your files, so press Use this drive as my backup and it becomes your backup in seconds. From then on, plug it in and press Back up now on the start screen: Techies Move adds what’s new or changed. If a file has changed, the copy from last time is kept in Earlier versions, and nothing on the stick is ever deleted. A Windows notification reminds you every week. To get files back, the same This is my NEW computer button puts a backup back, or just copy what you need off the stick.

Every week after

Back up now: one button, then unplug it

The start screen says when you last backed up and whether your backup drive is plugged in. Press Back up now, wait a moment, then unplug the stick and keep it away from the PC, so it can’t be harmed along with it. Want to back up a PC that isn’t moving anywhere? Choose Back up this PC to a USB stick on the start screen.

// WHAT SIZE OF STICK?

What size USB stick do I need to move to a new PC?

It depends on how many photos and videos you have: a PC of letters and a few hundred photos may need 16 GB, while years of phone videos can need a portable hard drive. Techies Move counts it for you before you buy anything: open it on the old PC, choose This is my OLD computer, and it says how much there is and the size of stick that’s enough. Untick Videos, say, and it works it out again.

If you’d like to keep the stick as your backup afterwards, buy the next size up, so there’s room to grow. A stick sold as 64 GB holds a little less than 64 GB in Windows, and the table allows for that.

A stick formatted as FAT32 (common on older or smaller ones) can’t hold a single file of 4 GB or more. Techies Move tells you if any of yours are that big.

// DOESN’T WINDOWS DO THIS?

Doesn’t Windows 11 move your files for you?

Not any more, unless you pay for OneDrive space. Windows Easy Transfer was left out of Windows 10, and in September 2026 Microsoft took away the PC-to-PC Migration it had added to Windows 11’s Windows Backup app. What’s left is Windows Backup, which brings your files back from OneDrive.

Techies Move

  • Moves everything through a USB stick or hard drive you plug in: no cloud space to pay for, however much you have.
  • Your files never go online, and the two PCs don’t need to be connected.
  • Takes your favourites from every browser, your Outlook files and signatures, and lists the passwords you’ll need.
  • Then keeps the stick as your backup, if you like. Free, from a firm in Dorset you can ring.

Windows Backup (OneDrive)

  • Backs your folders up to OneDrive, then brings them back when you sign in on the new PC.
  • The free OneDrive gives 5 GB; most people’s photos need more, which means paying Microsoft for more space.
  • Only files that have finished uploading come back.
  • Good if you already pay for Microsoft 365 and your files are in OneDrive. If they are, Techies Move says so and leaves them to OneDrive.

Paid programs such as Laplink PCmover can also move installed programs. Techies Move doesn’t: programs need installing fresh on the new PC, and Techies Move lists them and has Windows install the ones it can. You may be offered a paid move in the shop where you bought the new PC, too. Prefer us to do it? We move people’s files and set up new computers every week: see how we do it, or read our guide to every way to transfer files to a new computer.

// SAFE TO USE

Is it safe? Will it delete anything?

Your files

  • Nothing on the old PC is moved off it, changed or deleted: everything is copied. Keep the old PC as it is for a few weeks, until you’re sure everything’s there.
  • Nothing on the new PC is written over: if a different file with the same name is already there, both are kept.
  • Each file is copied under a temporary name and checked before it’s given its real one, so a stick pulled out half way never leaves a broken file. Start again and it carries on where it stopped.
  • Nothing is ever deleted from the stick, including when you uninstall Techies Move.

Your privacy

  • Your files go from one PC to the other on your own stick. Nothing is uploaded, to us or anyone.
  • Passwords are never read or copied: not your email’s, not your browser’s.
  • Techies Move doesn’t check in with us, count you, or send anything anywhere. The only web pages it opens are the ones you press a button for, such as your webmail.
  • The installer is digitally signed by 365 Techies Ltd, so Windows can check it’s really ours. It doesn’t update itself: new versions will be on this page.

A USB stick with your photos and letters on it is worth looking after: keep it somewhere safe, and if you’re giving the old PC away or recycling it, have it wiped properly first.

// HONEST LIMITS

What it can’t do (yet)

  • Programs can’t be copied from one PC to another; they need installing fresh. Techies Move lists every program on the old PC, and on the new one Windows can install many of them for you. Programs that come from an account, such as Microsoft Office, Microsoft 365 or your antivirus, are installed from that company’s website by signing in.
  • Passwords aren’t copied, on purpose. It lists the ones you’ll need and how to check them first; Chrome and Edge passwords come back when you sign in to the browser with sync on.
  • It needs a USB stick or external hard drive. It can’t yet move straight from one PC to the other over Wi-Fi or a cable.
  • Both computers need Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit). It won’t run on a Windows 7 or 8 PC. No Mac, iPhone or Android version.
  • It moves the folders of the person signed in to Windows. If two people have their own Windows accounts on the old PC, run it in each one.
  • Files that are only in OneDrive (shown with a cloud) aren’t copied: they come back when you sign in to OneDrive on the new PC.
  • Email: it brings Outlook’s data files, signatures and your account settings. Emails kept by your email company come back when you add the address. Thunderbird and Windows Mail accounts are listed, not moved.

// GOOD QUESTIONS

Frequently asked

How do I transfer files from my old computer to my new one?

Install Techies Move on both computers. On the old one, plug in a USB stick, choose This is my OLD computer and press Start copying. Then plug the stick into the new one, choose This is my NEW computer and press Put it on this PC. Your photos, documents, desktop, music, videos, downloads, favourites and Outlook files go into the right places. It’s free, and nothing is uploaded.

Does Windows 11 have a transfer tool?

Not one that copies PC to PC any more. Microsoft removed the PC-to-PC Migration from Windows 11’s Windows Backup app in September 2026. Windows Backup still backs your folders up to OneDrive and brings them back on the new PC, but the free OneDrive only holds 5 GB. Techies Move does the move through a USB stick instead, with no cloud space needed.

What happened to Windows Easy Transfer?

Windows Easy Transfer was left out of Windows 10, so it isn’t on Windows 10 or 11 PCs. Techies Move does the same job for Windows 10 and 11, free: it copies your files, favourites and Outlook files onto a USB stick and puts them back on the new PC.

Is there a free alternative to PCmover?

Yes: Techies Move is free and moves your folders, favourites, email files and signatures, and desktop picture, and lists your passwords to check. Laplink PCmover is a paid program that can also move installed programs; Techies Move lists your programs instead, and Windows can install many of them again on the new PC.

What size USB stick do I need to move to a new computer?

Open Techies Move on the old PC and choose This is my OLD computer: it counts your files and tells you, for example “This needs 41 GB, so a 64 GB USB stick or bigger is enough”. As a rough guide, up to about 12 GB fits a 16 GB stick, 25 GB a 32 GB stick, 50 GB a 64 GB stick and 100 GB a 128 GB stick; more than about 400 GB needs a portable hard drive. Buy a size up if you’ll keep the stick as your backup.

Will Techies Move copy my passwords?

No, on purpose: it never reads or copies passwords. It lists the email addresses and browser sign-ins you’ll need passwords for, with a button to check each email password in your webmail while the old PC still works. Chrome and Edge bring back saved passwords when you sign in with sync on; Techies Move tells you if yours are only on the old PC, and how to take them with Chrome’s own export.

Will my emails move to the new computer?

Emails kept by your email company (BT, Sky, Gmail, Outlook.com, Virgin Media and most others) come back as soon as you add your address in Outlook on the new PC: Techies Move lists each address with its settings and what to know. Emails of older POP accounts kept in Outlook data files (.pst) are copied across for you to open in Outlook, and classic Outlook’s signatures are put back too.

Will it move my programs?

Programs can’t be copied from one PC to another; they need installing fresh. Techies Move lists every program on the old PC, and on the new one it can have Windows install many of them again: you tick the ones you want. Microsoft Office, Microsoft 365 and antivirus are installed by signing in on that company’s website.

How do I move my Chrome bookmarks to a new computer?

If you sign in to Chrome with your Google account and sync is on, your bookmarks come back when you sign in on the new PC; Techies Move tells you which account. It also saves every browser’s favourites, including the ones Chrome keeps in your Google account, as a bookmarks file, and on the new PC shows you Chrome’s or Edge’s import page to bring them in.

Can I use the USB stick as a backup afterwards?

Yes. On the new PC, press Use this drive as my backup: the copy already on the stick becomes your backup. After that, press Back up now whenever the stick is plugged in, and Techies Move adds what’s new or changed, keeps the previous copy of any changed file, and never deletes anything from the stick. A Windows notification reminds you every week.

Will it delete anything from my old computer?

No. Everything is copied, not moved: the old PC keeps all its files, and nothing on it is changed. On the new PC nothing is written over either; a different file with the same name is kept alongside, with “(from old PC)” on its name.

Do I need to install it on both computers?

Yes, on the old one to pack up and on the new one to put it back. It’s a small, free download that needs no administrator password, and you can uninstall it afterwards without losing anything on the stick.

Does Techies Move work on Windows 7 or 8?

No. Both PCs need Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit). If your old PC runs Windows 7 or 8, copy your folders onto a USB stick by hand, or ring us and we’ll move them for you.

Is Techies Move really free?

Yes: no trial, no adverts, no account and no limit on how much it moves. If you’d rather we did the move for you, that’s a paid job, and we agree the price with you before we start.

Rather we did the move for you?

We move people’s photos, documents, email and favourites to new computers every week, remotely or at our Bournemouth base. It’s a paid job, and we agree the price before we start.
